<BigPicture title="The interview is a feed, not a printing press">

A strong news-feed answer is a traceable argument: who may see a post, how a post becomes a candidate, how those candidates are ranked, and which copies are allowed to lag. The interviewer should be able to point at any box and hear whether it is **authoritative** or **derived**, and what happens when a celebrity publishes, a user unfollows, or a page request races a new insert.

We will work one complete prompt:

> **Design a home news feed** that lets a user publish a post, follow and unfollow other users, and read a ranked timeline of people they follow.

The first architecture targets one product region for writes, hybrid fan-out, cursor pagination, and a read-time privacy filter. Marketplace checkout, live video, ads auctioning, and a globally linearizable feed are out of scope unless the interviewer adds them. Mechanisms for write amplification live in [fan-out architectures](/learn/fan-out-architectures); this lesson is the timed design that applies them.

### Requirements and invariants

- A caller can create a post, follow or unfollow, and page a home timeline.
- A post exists when the **post store** commits a sequenced row. Timeline membership is not that commit.
- Who may see a post is decided by the **current follow/block graph** (plus the post's visibility) at **read** time.
- Regular authors are pushed into follower inboxes. Authors over a celebrity threshold are **not** pushed.
- Ranking scores a candidate set. It does not mint posts and it is not the privacy gate.
- Pagination must not skip or duplicate items solely because a newer post landed at the head.
- Target p99 is 200 ms for a first home-timeline page under normal load, and 300 ms for publish ack after the post commit (fan-out may lag).

This is the “why” behind the architecture: the graph and post rows are the source of truth; inboxes, celebrity caches, rank feature caches, and unread counters are rebuildable views. TokenX notifications may copy that way. TokenX money must not.

<UnderTheHood title="Work the feed from numbers to hybrid reads">

### 1. Estimate the workload

Assume 500 million registered users, 50 million daily active viewers, 200 original posts/s, a mean of 400 followees, a 10× peak around a breaking event, and 80,000 home-timeline reads/s at peak. One tracked celebrity has 40 million followers and posts about once per minute. Celebrity skip threshold: 10,000 followers. Inbox payload: 200 B per derived row. These figures are **illustrative planning numbers**, not a measured product.

<Calculation label="News-feed planning envelope">
Mean push fan-out = 200 posts/s × 400 followers = 80,000 inbox writes/s

Inbox bytes at the mean = 80,000 writes/s × 200 B = 16 MB/s of derived appends, before indexes and replication

Celebrity push with no skip = 40,000,000 copies / 60 s ≈ 667,000 writes/s for that one post

Peak original posts at 10× = 2,000 posts/s; mean-style push would be 800,000 inbox writes/s if every author were regular

Home-timeline reads at peak = 80,000 reads/s. A naive pull of the last 10 posts from 400 followees is 32 million origin lookups/s unless recent-post lists are cached.

Retention: last 800 inbox rows × 200 B ≈ 160 KB per viewer; 50 million DAU ≈ 8 TB of hot derived inbox bytes before replicas
</Calculation>

The mean write budget is large but partitionable by `viewer_id`. The celebrity number is not: it is a **hot key** on one publish. Hybrid exists to keep the 80,000/s path and move the 40-million-follower tax onto a cached author list plus the read mixer. See [fan-out architectures](/learn/fan-out-architectures) for the same amplification math.

### 2. Define the API contract

- <code>POST /v1/posts</code> with body <code>{'{text, visibility}'}</code>. Returns <code>{'{postId, authorSeq, committedAt}'}</code> after the post row commits. Fan-out is not in the ack path.
- <code>POST /v1/follows/{'{userId}'}</code> records an idempotent graph edge and enqueues backfill. Completeness is a watermark, not the HTTP 200.
- <code>DELETE /v1/follows/{'{userId}'}</code> updates the graph first, then enqueues revoke of leftover inbox rows.
- <code>GET /v1/feed?cursor=...&limit=20</code> returns a page plus a next cursor. No integer <code>offset</code>.

Publish retries use an idempotency key scoped to the author. Follow retries must not create duplicate edges.

### 3. Model authoritative versus derived data

- <code>posts(post_id, author_id, body, visibility, author_seq, created_at)</code> — **authoritative** object and per-author sequence.
- <code>graph(follower_id, followee_id, edge, updated_at)</code> — **authoritative** follow and block edges.
- <code>inbox(viewer_id, post_id, author_id, sort_key)</code> — **derived** regular-follower materialization. Unique on <code>(viewer_id, post_id)</code>.
- <code>celebrity_recent(author_id, post_id, author_seq)</code> — **derived** skip-path cache.
- Rank features, rank scores, and unread counters — **derived**. Rebuildable from posts, graph, and engagement logs.

The fan-out worker consumes a post log keyed by `author_id` so one author's publishes stay in broker append order for that key. That is per-key order, not a global timeline; the contract is in [ordering and event keys](/learn/ordering-and-event-keys). Celebrity `author_id` is a hot key, which is why that path **skips** inbox push rather than partitioning harder.

### 5. Name the races the design must survive

**Backfill versus live push.** After Follow, a job copies the followee's last N posts into the new inbox up to watermark `(followee_id, last_copied_author_seq)`. Live fan-out can land the same `post_id`. Inserts are idempotent. Follow 200 means membership, not a finished history.

**Unfollow and privacy versus materialized copies.** Graph update is first. Every mix **filters** leftover inbox rows. A revoke job deletes by `(viewer_id, author_id)` and repairs unread counts. Until revoke finishes, storage still holds copies; the read filter is the privacy gate. The same race applies when a post flips from public to followers-only: already-pushed rows in non-follower inboxes remain until filter or revoke.

**Freshness versus ranking.** A newly committed post can appear at the head of a recency mix within fan-out lag, while a ranker that waits on features will omit it. Do not block publish on the ranker. Prefer a recency injection window (for example, last 60 s unscored but graph-filtered) plus a nearline ranker. Rank feature caches are derived: TTL, version fence, and stampede control belong with [caching patterns and invalidation](/learn/caching-patterns-and-invalidation). A 30 s stale rank cache is usually acceptable; a 30 s stale TokenX **balance** is not.

**Multi-region.** Place post and graph writes in one home region (or one owner per `post_id` / edge). Replica regions may serve labeled-stale celebrity caches and inbox tails. Do not accept conflicting writes to the same inbox key in two regions without a merge rule. Failover is a fenced cutover with a named RPO, not an instant safe flip; see [multi-region design](/learn/multi-region-design). TokenX spendable writes stay on their ledger region even if this feed is multi-region.

Overload: bound the fan-out queue. Shed **notifications and rank features** first. Do not drop post commit or graph writes to “keep the feed up.”

<MentalModel title="A porch stack, a celebrity display copy, and a librarian's ranking stamp">

Think of the post as one editorial page in a library. The follow graph is the subscription desk: it decides who is allowed to pick the page up. Regular fan-out is a printing press that drops a copy on each subscriber's porch (the inbox)—cheap to glance at, expensive if one columnist has forty million porches. Celebrity skip leaves a single display copy at the library that the door staff merge in. Ranking is a librarian stamping “read this first” on a pile of pages that were **already allowed**; the stamp is not the page and not the subscription card. Unfollow cancels the subscription at the desk while a paper already on the porch stays readable until someone takes it back. Pagination is a bookmark in that pile, not “page 3 of a binder that is still being stuffed.”

Retain this loop:

1. **Requirement:** What must users do, and who must never see a post after unfollow or a visibility change?
2. **Constraint:** What write amplification, read merge, freshness, and privacy lag follows?
3. **Decision:** Hybrid push for regular keys, skip for celebrities, filter on every read, cursor pages.
4. **Consequence:** Two serving paths, a revoke job, a ranker that can lag, and storage that is not the permission system.

For TokenX: a transfer may fan out **notification copies** the same way a post fans out inbox rows. The debit itself stays one ledger row. Copying spendable balances into N inboxes is the feed design applied to the wrong invariant.

</MentalModel>

<RealExamples title="Regular post, celebrity, unfollow, and TokenX notices">

### Regular author, hybrid push

User R has 800 followers and publishes. The post row commits with `author_seq = 412`. The worker writes 800 inbox rows keyed by `(viewer_id, post_id)`. Followers who open the feed mix those rows, rank, and page with a cursor. If the worker crashes after 300 inserts, replay inserts the remaining 500; the unique key prevents duplicates. Availability of the home timeline does not require all 800 copies to have landed: a follower whose row is late sees a **partial** feed until catch-up, which is lag, not loss of the post.

### Celebrity skip

User C has 40 million followers. The worker does **not** walk that list. It appends C's post to `celebrity_recent` and returns. Each follower of C already has C in the graph, so the mixer fetches that short cache (with stampede control on miss) and unions it. Pushing C would have been ~667,000 writes/s for one post and would not have made ranking or privacy any more correct.

### Unfollow after materialization

User A still has C's last post in a leftover inbox row (or will mix it from the celebrity cache). A unfollows C. The graph edge is removed. The next mix drops C's posts even if the inbox row and cache entry still exist. A revoke job later deletes A's leftover rows. Promising “the post is gone from every store in 0 ms” is not a design; it is a denial of distributed copies.

### TokenX notifications versus money

A completed TokenX transfer may fan out **notification** copies to devices on the two wallets. That path can be at-least-once and skipped under overload, same as feed notifications. The **ledger** remains one debit/credit with a unique event id. Other wallets' activity UIs may pull public receipt metadata; they must not assemble a spendable balance from fanned-out copies.

<InterviewTraps title="IC6 follow-ups and a defensible rubric">

### Questions the interviewer should be able to ask

- Where does a post become real, and what does publish ack actually guarantee?
- Why hybrid: push regular, skip celebrity—and what is the skip threshold in follower count and writes/s?
- Why is ranking derived, and what do you serve if the ranker or its cache is stale or down?
- User A unfollows B after B's post is already in A's inbox. What is visible on the next read, and what is still on disk?
- Why are pagination cursors required, and what breaks with `offset=40` during a breaking-news insert?
- How do you choose freshness versus ranking when a post is 5 s old and unscored?
- TokenX just committed a transfer. What may fan out, and what must not?
- What Kafka key orders the fan-out log, and why is celebrity `author_id` a hot key you skip rather than “partition more”?
- How would you place this in two regions without dual-writing the same inbox key?

### IC6 scoring rubric

- **Requirements:** separates posts and graph from inboxes, rank caches, and notifications; excludes unsupported product scope.
- **Quantification:** converts posts/s and follower degree into inbox writes/s, celebrity spike, read amplification, and storage.
- **Correctness:** states privacy as graph-at-read, idempotent inbox keys, backfill watermarks, and no exactly-once claim without a sink unique key.
- **Architecture:** hybrid fan-out, derived ranking, cursor pagination, dashed failure and revoke paths.
- **Operations:** queue lag, celebrity skip, cache stampede, revoke backlog, labeled stale rank pages, regional RPO.
- **Evolution:** delay global pull or full push until measurements require it; do not “fix” celebrity with more inbox shards.
- **Communication:** TokenX notifications may fan out; money does not. Revises when the interviewer adds live video or ads.

A weak answer says “fan-out on write to everyone, Redis ranks the only copy, unfollow deletes the post, page=2 is fine, and Kafka is exactly once.” A defensible answer names hybrid skip, derived ranking, the privacy race against already-materialized inboxes, cursors, and the freshness-versus-ranking product choice.

</InterviewTraps>

<Tradeoffs title="Why this design—and when it stops fitting">

**Hybrid fan-out:** keeps mean inbox writes around 80,000/s and avoids a 667,000/s celebrity spike, but you now operate two serving paths, a skip policy, and a mixer that must not forget the graph filter.

**Derived ranking and rank cache:** lets you change the model without rewriting posts, but pages can omit a brand-new post or show a slightly stale order. Blocking publish on ranking buys order quality and loses write availability.

**Filter-on-read plus async revoke:** privacy is correct on the next mix even when copies remain, but you pay a graph lookup on every request and you retain leftover bytes until the job runs. Eager inbox-only delete without a filter leaves a leak window.

**Cursor versus offset:** cursors survive head inserts and in-flight backfill; they make “jump to page 50” a different product. Feeds should not promise stable offsets.

**Single write region for posts and edges:** avoids conflicting inbox merge, but remote viewers pay replica lag. Active/active writes on the same `inbox` key without a merge rule create duplicate or resurrected copies; that is a [multi-region](/learn/multi-region-design) bug, not a latency win.

This design becomes a poor fit when almost every author is above the skip threshold (you have built pull), when the product requires a globally identical ranked page at the same wall-clock instant (you need a different consistency story), or when the interviewer actually wants a ledger. In that last case, stop drawing inboxes around TokenX balances.

</Tradeoffs>

<InterviewAnswer>

“I scope the news feed to publish, follow/unfollow, and a ranked home timeline. Authoritative state is sequenced posts plus the follow/block graph. Inboxes, celebrity recent-post lists, rank scores, and unread counters are derived. At 200 posts per second and a mean of 400 followers, push is about 80,000 inbox writes per second. A 40-million-follower celebrity would be on the order of 667,000 writes per publish, so I skip that key, keep an author cache, and merge it at read.

Ranking runs after the mixer filters candidates against the live graph. It never stores the only copy of a post. Follow records an idempotent edge and a watermarked backfill; Follow 200 is membership, not finished history. Unfollow updates the graph first. Already-materialized inbox rows can still exist, so every read filters, and a revoke job cleans storage. Pagination uses a cursor of sort key and post id; offsets skip and duplicate when a new row lands at the head.

I will inject a short recency window rather than block publish on the ranker; a stale rank cache is a freshness bound, not linearizability. Fan-out is keyed by author id for per-author order, and celebrity is a hot key I skip rather than reshard. TokenX may fan out notifications the same way; spendable balances stay on the ledger. If the fan-out queue saturates I shed notifications and rank features, not post commit.”

</InterviewAnswer>
