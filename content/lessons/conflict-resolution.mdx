<BigPicture title="Convergence is not the same as correctness">

When replicas accept writes without coordinating, concurrent versions are inevitable. Conflict resolution decides whether one wins, values merge, or a person repairs the result. A deterministic rule can make replicas converge and still lose money, resurrect deleted data, or violate an invariant.

Use merge semantics only when the domain has a meaningful join. Ad campaign labels, shopping-cart items, or observed counters can sometimes merge. TokenX balances cannot be merged by picking the largest value or adding replica totals; spend authorization requires a single invariant-preserving authority, escrow rights, or a transaction protocol.

</BigPicture>

<UnderTheHood title="Detect first, resolve second">

A scalar version from one leader totally orders its writes but cannot describe independent replica progress. A version vector records the greatest counter observed from each replica. Vector `{A:3, B:1}` dominates `{A:2, B:1}` because every component is at least as large and one is greater. `{A:3, B:1}` and `{A:2, B:2}` are concurrent: neither dominates the other.

### Resolution choices

- **Last-write-wins (LWW):** choose the greatest timestamp or version tuple. It is compact and convergent but depends on clock/order assumptions and silently discards concurrent intent.
- **Multi-value register:** retain concurrent siblings and resolve in application or UI. It preserves evidence but pushes complexity to readers.
- **Deterministic application merge:** combine independent fields or apply business precedence. It must be associative, commutative, and idempotent if merge order varies.
- **Conflict-free replicated data type (CRDT):** a data type whose operations or states converge under defined algebra. Grow-only sets and counters are simple; removal needs causal context or tombstones.

An observed-remove set removes only additions it has observed. A concurrent add may survive a remove. That can be right for collaborative tags and wrong for an access revocation. Security revocation generally needs centralized or monotonic-deny semantics, not a convenient add-wins set.

Metadata has cost. A version vector with one entry per device grows without bound; systems compact identities, use dotted vectors, or scope replicas. Tombstones also require a safe garbage-collection frontier proving no old replica can reintroduce deleted state.

</UnderTheHood>

<MentalModel title="Merge is constitutional law, not cleanup">

Two branches edit copies of a policy. Version metadata tells whether one edit included the other or whether they were independent. The constitution specifies which edits combine, which rule prevails, and which conflict needs human judgment. “Newest file wins” is merely one law—and often a bad one.

Retain: detect causality, choose domain semantics, preserve evidence, and prove convergence separately from business validity.

</MentalModel>

<RealExamples title="Mergeable facts and non-mergeable authority">

**Ad campaign configuration.** Concurrent edits to independent presentation fields can merge. A PAUSED status should usually dominate a concurrent cosmetic edit, but that rule must be explicit and audited. Budget changes may require serialization because combining two independent increases can overspend.

**Ad click counters.** A grow-only per-replica counter can merge by component-wise maximum and sum components. Retrying the same click still requires event deduplication; a CRDT counter does not identify duplicate business events.

**TokenX.** Wallet notes or notification preferences may merge. Ledger entries and spendable balances may not use LWW or generic CRDT merge. A concurrent debit conflict must be rejected, reserved, or ordered by the ledger authority.

<InterviewTraps title="Convergence claims that lose intent">

1. **“LWW keeps the latest write.”** It keeps the largest ordering value, which may not be the latest real event under skew.
2. **“CRDT means no conflicts.”** The conflict policy is embedded in the type; its semantics may still be wrong for the product.
3. **“Counters merge, so retries are safe.”** Transport duplication and logical event identity are separate from replica convergence.
4. **“Delete wins forever.”** Tombstones consume space and require a causal-stability rule before collection.
5. **“Version vectors totally order writes.”** Concurrent vectors are intentionally incomparable.
6. **“Merge the balances.”** Financial conservation is not a semilattice; enforce spend rights before accepting writes.
7. **“Hide conflict from users.”** For documents or configuration, silent resolution can destroy intent; repair may be a product feature.

</InterviewTraps>

<Tradeoffs title="Metadata, availability, and semantic risk">

- LWW is operationally simple but loses concurrent intent and inherits ordering assumptions.
- Multi-value siblings preserve evidence but increase read complexity and can multiply under sustained concurrency.
- CRDTs allow coordination-free convergence for suitable algebra, at the cost of metadata, tombstones, and sometimes surprising semantics.
- Central serialization simplifies non-mergeable invariants but reduces write availability during partitions.
- User-visible repair is honest and flexible, but adds product and support complexity.

</Tradeoffs>

<InterviewAnswer>

“I separate conflict detection from resolution. Version vectors can show that one version contains another or that writes are concurrent; timestamps alone cannot prove causality. Then I choose a domain rule: LWW for low-value replaceable state, multi-value siblings for explicit repair, or a CRDT only when its algebra matches product intent.

Convergence is not correctness. Campaign labels may merge, a pause can dominate cosmetic edits, but TokenX balances require ordered spend authorization rather than LWW or generic merge. I also design tombstone retention and obsolete-replica rejoin rules. See [consistency models](/learn/consistency-models), [time and ordering](/learn/time-and-ordering), and [data modeling](/learn/data-modeling-from-access-patterns).”

</InterviewAnswer>
