<BigPicture title="A passport is not a signed check">

TLS and mTLS answer **who is on the wire**. Application authorization answers **what that principal may do**. The TokenX ledger answers **whether money moved**. Mixing these three is the IC6 trap.

This lesson is conceptual: certificates, rotation, segmentation, zero trust versus perimeter, WAF-class L7 policy, volumetric versus application-layer overload, and secret rotation. It does **not** teach how to break TLS, write payloads, or bypass controls.

A private link or IP allowlist is a **routing constraint**. It is not a unique index. A WAF is a derived filter. DDoS defense is capacity and anycast, not exactly-once. [Service mesh](/learn/service-mesh-and-proxies) already used mTLS as identity; here we put that identity in a trust map.

</BigPicture>

<UnderTheHood title="Identity, then segmentation, then rotation without a cliff">

### Three questions on every TokenX debit

1. **Transport:** Did we complete TLS 1.3 with a name we trust (or mTLS with a workload SAN we trust)? If not, **fail closed**. Do not debit over plaintext “because we are in the VPC.”
2. **Authorization:** Is this principal allowed `debits:create` for this tenant? A valid mesh cert on a metrics scraper is still a no.
3. **Commit:** Unique `(tenant, transfer_id)` in the ledger transaction.

Ad Click public ingest lives in a more exposed zone: signed click tokens (capability with expiry), TLS to collectors, uniqueness on the log. Dashboards stay derived and stay off the public ingest zone.

### Perimeter versus zero trust

Perimeter thinking: VPN and a fat trusted LAN. Anything inside may call the ledger. That fails when a debug box shares the LAN.

Zero trust thinking: authenticate every hop, least privilege, assume the network is hostile. You still need **segmentation** so a compromised ingest collector cannot open a SQL session to the TokenX vault. Zones are blast-radius bounds, not identity.

Volumetric floods are [rate limiting](/learn/rate-limiting) plus capacity and anycast at the edge ([CDNs](/learn/cdns-and-edge-architecture)). Application-layer floods are expensive POSTs. TokenX fail-closes with 503/429 rather than forging 200. Ads ingest may shed bots and keep keyed billed clicks.

<Calculation label="Certificate overlap and DDoS occupancy (illustrative)">

Leaf cert lifetime 48 h, rotate at 24 h, overlap 24 h so verifiers accept old and new. If rotation is a hard cut at expiry with 5-minute clock skew, a replica with a slow clock rejects the new cert and fail-closes—the correct money behavior, but an **availability** incident you caused. Overlap is the recovery.

Volumetric 10 Gb/s at an edge that terminates TLS: 10e9 / 8 / 1500 ≈ **833k packets/s** if full MTU, worse if small packets. That is capacity planning, not an application unique-id problem. If 50k of those packets are POST /debits without keys, the API must 429/fail closed **before** the ledger pool fills ([backpressure](/learn/backpressure-and-overload)).

</Calculation>

Secret rotation (DB passwords, HMAC keys for click tokens) needs **dual-accept** then **drop old**. A click token HMAC hard-cut invalidates in-flight beacons; those clicks can retry with a new token **and the same click_id**. New click_id on rotation is a double-bill path.

<MentalModel title="Airport ID, boarding pass, then the bank">

TLS is checking a passport. Authorization is the boarding pass for that flight. The ledger is the bank at the destination. A valid passport for the wrong flight still does not move money. A private jetway (private link) is still not a bank receipt. Rotating passports with overlap means two valid IDs for a day, not two accounts.

Retain: **identity, authorization, and commit are three fences; segmentation limits blast radius; rotation uses overlap; fail closed on money if any fence is missing.**

</MentalModel>

**Authoritative:** IAM policy store you named; TokenX ledger; billed-fact log. **Derived:** cert bundles, WAF rules, allowlists, private-link mappings, edge counters. Solid edges are TLS, authorize, commit, and ingest that cannot route to the ledger. Dashed edges are fail-closed identity failure, missing lateral path from collectors, and derived dashboards. Recovery is overlap rotation, deny-by-default east-west, and never treat VPC membership as a debit grant.

</ArchitectureDiagram>

<RealExamples title="TokenX three fences; Ad Click exposed but unique">

**TokenX.** Internet-facing gateway: TLS 1.3, no 0-RTT, WAF for obvious malformed bodies (not a business-rule engine). East-west mTLS to the API. IAM on tenant scope. Ledger in a separate network zone; collectors and CI have no route. Private link from a bank partner is still mTLS + IAM + uniqueness.

**Ad Click.** Beacons hit a public collector zone. Tokens are HMAC-signed with rotating keys (dual-accept). Uniqueness is `click_id` on the log. A volumetric flood is shed at the edge; remaining clicks still unique. Compromised collector can lie about **unbilled** debug fields; it must not obtain ledger credentials.

<InterviewTraps title="Security slogans">

- **“We are in a VPC so TLS is optional.”** Identity still matters on the LAN.
- **“mTLS means authorized.”** Wrong question answered.
- **“Allowlists are non-repudiation.”** They are routing.
- **“WAF means the API is safe.”** It is a derived filter with false positives.
- **“Cert rotation is fencing.”** Fencing tokens are in [distributed locks](/learn/distributed-locks-and-leases). Certs overlap; they do not fence a debit.
- **“Zero trust means no segmentation.”** You still bound blast radius.

Do not claim a perimeter or TLS configuration is unbreakable. State the fences you actually check.

</InterviewTraps>

<Tradeoffs title="Fewer public packets versus more fences to operate">

- **Private connectivity versus public TLS:** smaller attack surface versus partner operational cost; not fewer application fences.
- **Short-lived certs versus long:** steal window versus rotation load.
- **WAF strictness versus false 403s:** safety versus availability of honest clients.
- **Collecting ads in an exposed zone versus one flat network:** blast radius versus extra hops.

</Tradeoffs>

<InterviewAnswer>

“I split three fences: TLS or mTLS for transport identity, IAM for authorization, and the TokenX ledger unique transfer_id for commit. A VPC or private link is a routing constraint, not a receipt. Segmentation exists so a compromised Ad Click collector has no path to the vault. Ads ingest can sit in a more exposed zone with signed tokens and uniqueness on the billed log; dashboards stay derived. I rotate certs and HMACs with dual-accept overlap so expiry is not a cliff and not a fencing token. If identity cannot be established on a debit, I fail closed. I do not claim TLS authenticates business operations beyond the wire, and I do not teach or rely on bypass folklore.”

</InterviewAnswer>
