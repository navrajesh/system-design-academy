<BigPicture title="Every unique label set is a new time series">

Prompt: **Design a metrics platform** (ingest, store, query, alert) for product and infra telemetry.

A **time series** is a metric name plus a label set. Cardinality is combinatorial: `http_requests{path,code,pod,user_id}` with unbounded `user_id` will not fit in memory. This lesson composes [time-series storage](/learn/time-series-and-append-only-storage), [profiling and observability](/learn/profiling-and-observability), and [cost-performance](/learn/cost-performance-engineering).

TokenX exposes ledger commit latency and outbox age as **bounded-label** series. Ad Click may emit campaign-level rates; per-click series are a billing log, not Prometheus.

</BigPicture>

<UnderTheHood title="Cardinality math, then the write and query split">

### Envelope (illustrative)

10,000 pods × 50 metrics × 20 label combinations = 10 million series. 15 s scrape. 2 bytes compressed per sample (optimistic). 15-day raw retention, then 1-minute rollups for 1 year.

<Calculation label="Samples and storage">
Samples/s = 10e6 series / 15 s ≈ 667,000 samples/s

Raw retention samples = 667,000 × 15 × 86,400 ≈ 8.64e11 samples

At 2 B/sample ≈ 1.73 TB compressed raw for 15 days, before indexes, replication, and replication of query caches

If someone adds unbounded user_id with 1e6 values, series become 10e6 × 1e6 — that is not a scrape-interval tune
</Calculation>

### Architecture

- **Ingest:** agents or remote-write; bounded labels; drop or aggregate high-cardinality at the edge.
- **Write path:** append-only chunks in TSDB; recent data hot in memory; WAL for crash.
- **Rollups:** recording rules or downsampling jobs — **derived**.
- **Query:** fan-out to shards by metric or time; timeouts and partial results must be labeled ([tail latency](/learn/tail-latency-control)).
- **Alerting:** evaluates recording rules on a schedule. Alert history is not the TSDB source of truth for raw samples.
- **Exemplars / traces:** pointers into traces; missing a complete trace is not “the counter lied.”

Do not use a TokenX serializable transaction per sample. Durability is WAL+replication of the TSDB, with an explicit loss window on crash if you run a single replica.

<RealExamples title="User-id explosion, TokenX RED, Ad Click">

**Unbounded label.** A developer adds `user_id`. Memory and compaction fall over. Fix is drop+quota, not “add Kafka partitions.”

**TokenX RED.** `tokenx_transfer_commit` with `tenant_tier,result` — tens of series, not per-wallet.

**Ad Click.** Per-campaign rates OK if campaign count is bounded; per-click metrics belong on the billed log.

<Tradeoffs title="Fidelity versus bill">

**Long raw retention:** debug power; SSD and compaction cost.

**Aggressive rollup:** cheap; blinds short incidents.

**High scrape rate:** better resolution; linear sample cost.

**Per-tenant quotas:** fairness; angry developers.

</Tradeoffs>

<InterviewAnswer>

“I start from series count: name times label combinations times scrape rate. I bound labels at ingest with quotas. A TSDB keeps raw samples for a short debug window and derived rollups for long query. Alerting is a consumer that must not treat partial queries as zero. TokenX and Ad Click get low-cardinality RED metrics; per-click or per-wallet series belong in logs or the ledger, not in the TSDB.”

</InterviewAnswer>
