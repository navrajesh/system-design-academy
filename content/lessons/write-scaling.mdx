<BigPicture title="Write throughput is ownership, not replica count">

Read scaling in [Scaling read-heavy services](/learn/scaling-read-heavy-services) multiplies copies of **derived** state. Write scaling multiplies **owners** of **authoritative** state. Extra API processes do not increase durable throughput if they all lock the same ledger row, increment the same sequence, or hash to the same partition.

A write path has three different verbs that interviews collapse into “just persist it”:

- **Acknowledge** a caller (availability of the API).
- **Commit** bytes the product is willing to lose money or invoices over (durability).
- **Project** aggregates, caches, and search indexes (derived views).

TokenX spendable money lives in the ledger plus a unique `(tenant, transfer_id)` fence. You cannot write-behind a debit into a cache and call it committed. Ad Click billed facts may batch in memory **only until** they hit a durable log; collector ACK is then a durability claim. Windowed campaign totals remain derived and rebuildable.

Cross-lesson spine: how you **place** keys is [Partitioning vs sharding vs aggregation](/learn/partitioning-vs-sharding-vs-aggregation); how retries stay safe is [Idempotency and deduplication](/learn/idempotency-and-deduplication); pairing a database commit with a log is [Transactions across boundaries](/learn/transactions-across-boundaries) and [Kafka delivery and failure](/learn/kafka-delivery-and-failure); per-key order is [Ordering and event keys](/learn/ordering-and-event-keys).

</BigPicture>

<UnderTheHood title="Batch, own the key, then name what the ACK promised">

### Batching: latency paid, amplification bought, retries multiplied

A batch amortizes fsync, RPC, and index maintenance across *N* items. It also holds the first item until the *N*th arrives or a linger timer fires, and it turns one failed ACK into *N* retries.

Planning assumptions (illustrative, not a measured cluster): TokenX transfer API at 2,000 commits/s, 800 B encoded facts, linger 5 ms or 50 records. Ad Click collector peak 80,000 clicks/s, 400 B billed facts, linger 20 ms or 500 records.

<Calculation label="Batch linger versus retry blast radius">

TokenX: at 2,000 transfers/s a 50-record batch fills in 50 / 2,000 = 0.025 s. A 5 ms linger usually wins, so p50 extra wait ≈ 5 ms, not 25 ms.

Ad Click: 80,000 clicks/s × 400 B = 32 MB/s leader payload before headers and compression. A 500-record batch is 200 KB; fill time = 500 / 80,000 = 6.25 ms. A 20 ms linger adds up to 20 ms to the first click in a sparse window.

Retry: one timed-out 500-click batch re-sends 500 facts. Without a stable click id those 500 can double-count a derived window. With an idempotent billed-fact log they are duplicates of identity, not of money.

</Calculation>

Batching is a throughput lever. It is not a correctness lever. The correctness lever is the identity that survives the retry.

### Partitioned ownership and single-writer keys

If a key must be applied in a total order—TokenX account *A* debit then credit—**one owner at a time** must serialize that key. Mechanisms that actually provide it: a per-account partition with a single consumer (or single-writer replica), a row lock on `accounts[A]`, or an explicit sequencer whose output is the apply order.

Mechanisms that **do not**: more write replicas of the same primary, sticky sessions without fencing, or hashing transfers by `transfer_id` while still needing per-account order. Hashing by transfer spreads load and **destroys** account order; hash by `account_id` preserves order and **creates** a celebrity-account hot partition. See [Ordering and event keys](/learn/ordering-and-event-keys).

### Synchronous ACK versus asynchronous write

A **sync ACK** means the caller may treat the write as durable **inside the named store**: TokenX ledger transaction committed; billed-click record below the log’s high-watermark under the delivery lesson’s `acks` / ISR policy. An **async ACK** means the API accepted work into a buffer or queue. That is availability. It is not durability. The loss window is “process crash, replica failover, or disk not yet flushed” until the authoritative commit.

**Write-behind** is the extreme async case: mutate a cache, ACK, flush later. The loss window is every write not yet in the WAL or database. That is legal for TokenX **token metadata** and for ad **debug counters**. It is forbidden for TokenX **debits** and for **billed click facts**.

**Write-ahead log (WAL) versus cache:** the WAL (or Kafka fact log, or database redo) is the authority until retention. A cache is a photocopy. Rebuilding the cache from the WAL is recovery. Rebuilding the WAL from the cache is fiction.

### Contention: hot partition, row lock, sequence generator

Three classic serial choke points:

- **Hot partition:** one `account_id` or `campaign_id` owns a disproportionate rate. Extra consumers cannot split a Kafka partition; extra SQL writers queue on the same row lock.
- **Row lock:** `UPDATE balances SET amount = amount - 100 WHERE account = A` serializes all of *A*. Ten API boxes become a longer wait queue, not 10× throughput.
- **Sequence generator:** a global `SELECT nextval('ledger_seq')` or a single-row counter is one lock. Prefer per-account sequences, UUID/ULID identities, or Hi/Lo ranges **if** the product does not need a single global integer order.

### Sharding writes, CDC fans, outbox, aggregation

**Shard writes** by the ownership key, not by “whatever is unique.” TokenX shards by account (or wallet) so each shard serializes that account. Cross-account transfers are two local mutations plus a protocol from [Transactions across boundaries](/learn/transactions-across-boundaries)—not one row on a global primary.

**Outbox:** the same local transaction inserts the ledger row and an outbox row. A publisher then fans to Kafka. The ledger remains the money authority; the topic is a **derived, at-least-once** changelog until the sink is idempotent. Dual-write without an outbox (DB then naked `produce`) can commit money and lose the event, or the reverse.

**CDC fans:** change-data capture tails the WAL and fans to search, warehouses, and caches. Fan-out is asynchronous. Derived stores lag. They must not be consulted as spendable balances.

**Per-event persist versus aggregate-then-persist:** billed clicks persist **per fact** (or per durable batch of facts) because finance reconstructs invoices from identities. Campaign dashboards may **aggregate then persist** a 1-minute window sum. If you only store the sum, you cannot prove a click, cannot replay a dispute, and cannot correct a hot-key double count except by guessing.

<InteractiveCalculation mode="little-law" title="In-flight writes versus the one locked row" />

Little’s Law here is the lock queue, not the API fleet. If 400 transfers/s hit celebrity account *A* and each holds the row 15 ms, *A* needs L = 400 × 0.015 = 6 overlapping lock waits on **one** row. Adding API replicas increases arrivals, not lock capacity. Queueing math for the rest of the path lives in [Latency, throughput, and queues](/learn/latency-throughput-and-queues).

</UnderTheHood>

<MentalModel title="One cashier per account, a numbered receipt, a vault log">

Picture a bank branch. Each account has **one cashier** (partition owner / row lock). Ten extra tellers standing behind the same cashier do not count cash faster. The customer’s **receipt number** is `(tenant, transfer_id)`: handing it twice must return the same outcome, not a second debit. The **vault log** (WAL / billed-fact topic) is what auditors believe. Sticky notes on the counter (write-behind cache, dashboard windows) can blow away in a draft.

A courier van (batch) is cheaper per envelope and slower for the first envelope. If the van crashes and you resend every envelope without receipt numbers, the vault double-books.

Retain: **own the key, fence the identity, ACK only what committed, treat aggregates as derived, and never confuse extra writers with extra lock capacity.**

</MentalModel>

**Authoritative state:** TokenX uniqueness `(tenant, transfer_id)` plus ledger rows, and the Ad Click **billed-fact log** until retention. The WAL (database redo or Kafka log) is the recovery source. **Derived state:** CDC-fed search/warehouse, window aggregators, dashboards, and any write-behind cache. **Consistency boundary:** a TokenX debit is committed only when the unique insert and balance mutation commit in one local transaction; collector ACK is legal only after billed facts are on the durable log. Per-account order is the **single owner** of that account key, not the load balancer. Solid edges are sync admission and commit. Dashed edges are reject (duplicate 409), forbidden write-behind for money, CDC/window asynchrony, and the cache’s loss window until a later flush. Recovery is retry the same idempotency key, rebuild derived views from the WAL/log, and never reconstruct spendable money from a cache.

<RealExamples title="TokenX serializes money; Ad Click durables facts then derives windows">

**TokenX.** Partition (or shard) by `account_id`. Serial ownership per account is mandatory if the product promises per-account debit/credit order. Uniqueness is `(tenant, transfer_id)` stored atomically with the mutation—see [Idempotency and deduplication](/learn/idempotency-and-deduplication). A celebrity treasury account at an illustrative 400 of 2,000 transfers/s is a hot key: scale **other** accounts by adding shards; this account still has one cashier. Write-behind of a debit is an integrity bug: crash-before-flush mints or loses spendable money. Analytics may follow an outbox; a produce timeout must not rewind the ledger. Cross-account settlement is a saga with local fences, not a second writer on both rows at once ([Transactions across boundaries](/learn/transactions-across-boundaries)).

**Ad Click Aggregator.** Collectors may **batch** 500 clicks, but they ACK the SDK only after the billed-fact batch is on the durable log (`acks` policy as in [Kafka delivery and failure](/learn/kafka-delivery-and-failure)). Aggregation windows (1-minute campaign totals, unique users) are **derived**: they can be rebuilt, sampled, or lagged under [Graceful degradation](/learn/graceful-degradation). A viral campaign is a hot partition if you key by `campaign_id`; collector keys spread ingest and move skew into the aggregator. Billing from the window table without the fact log cannot survive a double-applied batch.

<InterviewTraps title="Write-scaling slogans that fail at IC6">

- **“More writers mean more throughput.”** Not on one row, one sequence, or one partition. That is a longer queue.
- **“Async ACK is just as durable if we replicate the cache.”** Replication of derived state does not create a WAL. Loss window remains until the authority commits.
- **“Write-behind is fine; Redis has AOF.”** Redis AOF is a different durability envelope with its own fsync policy. It is still not the TokenX ledger and not an invoiceable click identity.
- **“Exactly-once because we batch.”** Batching multiplies duplicates on retry. Exactly-once is at best **effect-once inside a named atomic boundary** with a retained idempotency key—not a transport property.
- **“CDC is strongly consistent with the primary.”** CDC is asynchronous. Replica and search lag are expected. Spendable reads must not cross that boundary.
- **“We’ll aggregate clicks in memory and flush sums every minute to save Kafka.”** You saved brokers and destroyed billed facts. Disputes cannot replay a sum.
- **“Global AUTO_INCREMENT keeps transfers ordered.”** It orders inserts on that generator, serializes the whole fleet, and still does not equal per-account causal order across shards.
- **“Sticky sessions give us single-writer.”** Sticky is a routing hint. Failover, rolling deploys, and a second region create split writers unless you fence.

</InterviewTraps>

<Tradeoffs title="What you spend to go faster on writes">

- **Batch size versus latency and retry radius:** larger batches raise throughput and the cost of a timeout. TokenX small batches protect debit tail latency; ads can linger longer if ACK waits for the log.
- **Sync ACK versus availability:** sync ACK fails closed with the store; async ACK stays up and hides a loss window. Money and billed facts take sync-to-authority. Debug counters may not.
- **Per-event facts versus aggregate-then-persist:** facts cost storage and buy replay. Aggregates cost less and cannot reconstruct identity.
- **Account sharding versus global order:** shards scale unrelated keys and give up a single ledger sequence. Cross-shard transfers need a protocol, not a hope.
- **Single-writer order versus parallelism:** serial apply preserves invariants and caps that key’s QPS at 1 / service-time. Parallel apply needs commutative events or conflict resolution.
- **Outbox/CDC complexity versus dual-write luck:** outbox adds a publisher and lag; dual-write is simpler until the first crash.

Correctness of TokenX balances and billed-click identity is not a throughput knob. Completeness of a dashboard is.

</Tradeoffs>

<InterviewAnswer>

“I scale writes by adding owners of keys, not processes on one row. TokenX partitions by account so one owner serializes that account; uniqueness is (tenant, transfer_id) in the same transaction as the debit. I never write-behind spendable money—an ACK means the ledger committed, not that a cache will flush in 50 ms. Extra APIs on a celebrity row only grow lock wait; throughput stays about one over hold time, same as a global sequence generator. Ads may batch to amortize fsync, but the collector ACKs after billed facts are on a durable log; one-minute windows are derived and rebuildable, so a retried batch needs click ids or it double-counts. Outbox plus CDC fans search and dashboards asynchronously; those views are not balances. Batching buys amplification and spends latency and retry blast radius. Async ACK is availability, not durability. I name the WAL, the fence, the owner, and the loss window before I draw more boxes.”

</InterviewAnswer>
