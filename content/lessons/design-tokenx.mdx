<BigPicture title="The interview is conservation under ambiguity">

This mock is the full TokenX prompt. [Driving a System Design Interview](/learn/driving-a-system-design-interview) already walked the 45-minute loop on the same product. Here you must survive follow-ups: cross-shard terminals, fencing, restore, and a Web3 comparison that does not smuggle linearizability.

Prompt: **Design TokenX**, a custodial service that creates transfers, reads spendable balances, and lists history. One successful transfer debits and credits **exactly once inside the ledger transaction** (or an equivalent exclusive-terminal protocol). Total supply is conserved. A wallet cannot spend more than `available`.

**In scope:** authenticated `POST /v1/transfers` with `Idempotency-Key`, status reads, authoritative balances, cursor history, regional DR.

**Out of scope unless asked:** public-chain consensus, AMM trading, smart-contract custody, multi-region active-active last-write-wins balances.

Uniqueness is `UNIQUE (tenant_id, operation, idempotency_key)` plus a request hash. It is not a global UUID slogan. See [idempotency](/learn/idempotency-and-deduplication) and [transactions across boundaries](/learn/transactions-across-boundaries).

</BigPicture>

<UnderTheHood title="Numbers, one-region ledger, then the shard fork">

### 1. Capacity (illustrative, labeled)

Assume 50 million wallets, 20 million transfers/day, 20× market peak, 1 KB durable transfer+ledger bytes before indexes, 10 balance reads per transfer.

<Calculation label="TokenX planning envelope">
Average writes = 20,000,000 / 86,400 ≈ 231 transfers/s

Peak writes ≈ 4,630 transfers/s

Peak authoritative reads ≈ 46,300/s

Raw growth ≈ 20 GB/day; one year ≈ 7.3 TB before indexes, RF, and backups
</Calculation>

At this peak, a quorum-backed relational or distributed-SQL ledger with partitioned tables is simpler than a homemade cross-shard money protocol. Shard only when **measured** lock wait or commit p99 says so. Virtual `wallet_bucket` keys now; protocol later.

### 2. Authoritative schema

- `wallet_balances(wallet_id, asset, available, version)` — spend authorization.
- `transfers(tenant_id, idempotency_key, request_hash, transfer_id, status)` — command identity.
- `ledger_entries(transfer_id, account_id, asset, delta, sequence)` — balanced immutable lines.
- `outbox(...)` — committed integration intent in the **same** database transaction.

Same-region transfer: one serializable transaction, deterministic wallet lock order, then outbox. Kafka is **after** commit. RF and Kafka producer assumptions for the integration log: RF=3, `acks=all`, `min.insync.replicas=2`, idempotent producer, ISR-only election ([Kafka delivery](/learn/kafka-delivery-and-failure)).

### 3. Timeout is not failure

A lost 200 after commit is **UNKNOWN to the client**. Replay the same tenant-scoped key. Auto-replay of a signed **request log** after restore is forbidden: replay only **committed journals**, then fence writes ([backup and DR](/learn/backup-restore-and-disaster-recovery)).

### 4. Cross-shard exclusive terminals

When two wallets leave one lock domain, conservation is a saga, not two independent UPDATEs.

- Shard A: unique insert `RESERVED` on `transfer_id`, then exclusive `FINALIZED | RELEASED`.
- Shard B: unique row `ABSENT → CREDITED | CANCELLED`. Credit and cancel race; the winner is terminal. Delayed credit after `CANCELLED` is **rejected**. Delayed cancel after `CREDITED` cannot reverse the credit.
- Coordinator timeouts persist `UNRESOLVED` and **query B**. Never treat a lost RPC as cancel.

Fencing: a resource retains a durable high-water epoch; lease expiry is not immediate exclusion ([locks and leases](/learn/distributed-locks-and-leases)). Raft reads need current-term ReadIndex or a bounded lease, not “I once committed a no-op” ([consensus](/learn/consensus-and-leader-election)).

Retain: **invariant first, mechanism second, vendor third.** “Use Kafka” is not conservation. “Use blockchain” is not linearizability.

<RealExamples title="Hot wallet, delayed credit, and a fake chain shortcut">

**Hot exchange wallet.** Hash sharding does not split one wallet's serial invariant. Batch deposits, sub-balances with explicit semantics, or a single-writer queue. Each changes freshness.

**Delayed credit after cancel.** B already wrote `CANCELLED`. A late credit must lose the compare-and-set. Releasing A is then safe. The reverse (credit then cancel) keeps `CREDITED` and finalizes A.

**Interviewer offers LWW multi-region balances.** Last-write-wins can erase a debit. That is not TokenX. Use regional ownership, escrow, or a globally consistent protocol.

<InterviewTraps title="IC6 follow-ups">

- Where is conservation enforced during quorum loss?
- Why is uniqueness tenant-scoped?
- What does a fencing token survive that a TTL does not?
- Why is ReadIndex or a lease required for follower/leader reads?
- When do you shard, and which protocol preserves supply?
- Why is a signed HTTP log insufficient after restore?
- How is a public chain different from this ledger (finality, reorg, custody)?

Weak answers: “Kafka exactly-once,” independent balance writes, or “blockchain so we do not need transactions.”

</InterviewTraps>

<Tradeoffs title="Why this design, and when it stops">

**Serializable one-region ledger:** simplest proof; write availability tracks quorum.

**Exclusive-terminal saga:** scales lock domains; adds `UNRESOLVED` operations and query-on-timeout.

**Outbox + Kafka:** decouples notify/analytics; at-least-once and lag are mandatory.

**Fail-closed writes:** protects money; Ad Click billed ingest may fail-open locally ([graceful degradation](/learn/graceful-degradation)). Do not share one bulkhead.

Wrong when regulators forbid one region, or measured commit capacity is exhausted without an invariant-safe migration ([migration at scale](/learn/migration-at-scale)).

</Tradeoffs>

<InterviewAnswer>

“I scope TokenX to custodial transfers, spendable balances, and history in one authoritative region. Invariants are conservation, no overspend, and one effect per tenant-scoped idempotency key plus request hash. At 20 million transfers per day and a 20× peak I plan about 4.6 thousand writes per second.

Same-wallet-domain transfers use one serializable ledger transaction: claim the key, lock wallets in a stable order, write balanced entries, commit the outbox. Timeouts replay the key. Kafka is at-least-once after commit and never authorizes spending.

If I must split shards, A reserves uniquely, B is exclusively CREDITED or CANCELLED, and a coordinator timeout stays UNRESOLVED until B is queried. I fail closed on ledger quorum loss, fence restored replicas, and I would not shard until measurements require an invariant-safe protocol.”

</InterviewAnswer>
