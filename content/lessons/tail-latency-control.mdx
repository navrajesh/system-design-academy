<BigPicture title="Users feel the slowest sibling, not the average">

Mean latency is a planning number. Users and SLOs live in the **tail**: p95, p99, p99.9. A parent request that **fans out** to *N* backends waits on the **max of N**, not the mean of N. One slow shard, lock wait, GC pause, or overloaded replica becomes the whole page.

[Latency, throughput, and queues](/learn/latency-throughput-and-queues) already showed that utilization and unbounded queues **create** tails. This lesson is how you **cut** them: end-to-end **deadlines**, **cancellation that actually stops work**, **hedged / speculative** requests with a duplicate-load budget, and **partial results** with a named freshness bound.

Request fan-out here is “one user call becomes N RPCs.” Social **write fan-out** (copy a post into inboxes) is a different amplification tax in [Fan-out architectures](/learn/fan-out-architectures). Do not mix the two math envelopes.

Authoritative TokenX money is the ledger plus unique `(tenant, transfer_id)`. Authoritative Ad Click invoices are billed facts on the durable log. Dashboards, replica reads, and “8 of 10 shards returned” are **derived or partial**. Hedging a debit without the same identity is a double-spend path. Hedging a dashboard read with stated replica lag is a latency path.

</BigPicture>

<UnderTheHood title="Max of N, then stop work, then maybe send a second copy">

### p99 versus mean, then the max-of-N trap

Illustrative (not a measured cluster): TokenX balance page fans out to 8 account shards; each shard mean service 12 ms, p50 10 ms, p99 80 ms, independent given no shared lock. Ad Click operator dashboard fans out to 20 campaign partitions; each p99 100 ms.

The **mean** of eight 12 ms shards is still ~12 ms of service. The **parent** waits until the last shard returns (plus merge). If tails are independent, the chance the parent sees at least one child at or beyond that child’s p99 is `1 - 0.99^N`.

<Calculation label="Parent tail is not the child mean">

Eight shards: `1 - 0.99^8` ≈ 7.7% of parent requests include at least one p99 child. Twenty partitions: `1 - 0.99^20` ≈ 18%.

To keep the **parent** at p99 of the child distribution, each child must hit a higher percentile: `0.99 = F^N` so `F = 0.99^(1/N)`. For N = 8, F ≈ 0.9987 (about child p99.87). Shipping “our p99 is 80 ms” as the page SLO when the page is max-of-8 is a lie.

Hedge budget (same envelope): if you wait until child p95 = 40 ms then issue a second copy, extra offered load ≈ 5% (the mass slower than 40 ms), not 2×. Immediate hedge on every call is 2× load into [load-balancing](/learn/load-balancing-deep-dive) pools and the ledger.

</Calculation>

Shared failure (one hot lock, one GC, one AZ) **correlates** tails. Independence is the optimistic bound. A celebrity TokenX account that serializes on one row makes all “fan-out” siblings wait on the **same** cashier; hedging that row just doubles lock waiters.

### Deadlines are a budget; the server does not share them by default

A **client deadline** (for example 200 ms remaining) is a clock on the caller. HTTP/gRPC timeouts often only **abandon the socket**. The handler, the database session, and the Kafka produce can keep running. That is **orphaned work**: capacity spent after the user already got 504.

A real deadline **propagates**: remaining time in a header or gRPC deadline, `statement_timeout` / query cancel on the ledger, worker abort, and refuse to start a sibling RPC whose worst-case service already exceeds the leftover budget. [Load-balancing deep dive](/learn/load-balancing-deep-dive) drain timeouts are the same idea at deploy time: do not RST in-flight money, and do not let abandoned work occupy the pool forever.

**Cancellation that stops work** uses the data-plane cancel: gRPC `CANCEL`, Postgres `pg_cancel_backend` / `statement_timeout`, HTTP/2 RST_STREAM **and** handler abort, Kafka consumer stop-fetch for that request. **Orphaned queries** are what you get from only closing the inbound connection. They still hold locks, pool slots, and CPU. Queueing cost is the latency lesson: extra in-flight L = (orphan rate) × remaining service time.

Assumptions: 2,000 TokenX API req/s; 10% miss a 200 ms deadline; those handlers continue 1.5 s. Extra concurrency ≈ 200/s × 1.5 s = **300** orphaned in-flight calls—on top of the healthy load. Cancellation that actually stops work returns that 300 to the pool.

Retries after a timeout are **UNKNOWN** in [Retries, dead letters, and replay](/learn/retries-dead-letters-and-replay): the first attempt may have committed. Hedging is a **concurrent** second attempt, not a later retry, and it has the same uniqueness rule.

### Hedged and speculative copies need one identity

- **Hedged request:** after a delay (or immediately, at 2× cost), send the **same** logical operation to a second replica, AZ, or handler. First success wins; **cancel the loser**.
- **Speculative execution:** start the second copy before you have evidence the first is slow (always-on hedge, or prefetch of a likely next key). Duplicate load is the default, not the tail tax.

Both multiply **offered** QPS into the tail you were trying to cut. They are legal when the effect is **read-only** or **idempotent on a caller-owned key**. They are not a substitute for [Idempotency and deduplication](/learn/idempotency-and-deduplication).

**Request hedging versus idempotency:** a hedge is two in-flight attempts of one user operation. Both **must** carry the same idempotency key. For TokenX that key is `(tenant, transfer_id)` inserted in the same ledger transaction as the debit. If the hedge mints a new UUID, you have two transfers, not one faster transfer. Idempotent producer session ids on Kafka do **not** cover two HTTP POSTs with different `transfer_id`s.

**TokenX debit: hedge must not double-spend.** You may hedge a **read** of a replica with [replication and failover](/learn/replication-and-failover) lag named (for example “applied LSN ≥ token, else primary”). You may **not** hedge `POST /debits` unless both copies share `transfer_id` **and** uniqueness is inserted in the **same ledger transaction** as the debit. Two racing inserts without a unique constraint can both debit. Fail closed if the uniqueness store is down—same money rule as [Graceful degradation](/learn/graceful-degradation).

**Ad Click:** you **may** hedge a **read** of a derived dashboard against a lagging replica and label staleness (for example “windows as of ≤ 15 s replica apply lag”). You **must not** double-append billed facts: a hedged collector POST without a stable `click_id` becomes two invoiceable rows. Hedge the read path; identity-fence the write path.

### Partial results are a contract, not a timeout

Returning 7 of 8 shards, a sampled campaign chart, or a replica that is 400 ms behind is allowed when the product **names** incompleteness. Returning HTTP 200 with a fabricated full portfolio, or ACK of a billed batch that only hit 3 of 5 collectors, is a silent contract change. Partial **reads** of derived views; never partial **commits** of money or billed identity.

<InteractiveCalculation mode="little-law" title="Hedge amplification is extra in-flight work" />

Little’s Law at the **shard pool** boundary: if you immediate-hedge 2,000 reads/s, the pool sees ~4,000 arrivals. At 80 ms hold on the slow copy, that is extra concurrency the latency lesson already warned you about. Prefer delayed hedges so only the tail duplicates.

</UnderTheHood>

<MentalModel title="A taxi dispatch with one trip number, not two meters">

Picture a dispatcher who needs the **slowest** of eight taxis to arrive before the party can leave (max of N). The mean taxi time is a brochure number. A **deadline** is “leave without the last taxi at 200 ms.” **Cancellation** is radioing that last taxi to **stop driving**, not hanging up while it still burns fuel (orphaned query). A **hedge** is sending a second taxi for the late one, with the **same trip number** so the passenger is not billed twice. A **partial result** is departing with seven people and a sign that seat 8 is missing—not pretending the van was full, and not charging a second fare.

**Authoritative state:** TokenX ledger plus unique `(tenant, transfer_id)`; Ad Click **billed-fact log** (not this diagram’s replica). **Derived state:** replica copies, dashboard windows, partial shard merges. **Consistency boundary:** a debit commits only in the ledger transaction with the uniqueness row; a hedged debit is two attempts of **one** `transfer_id`, not two ids. Solid edges are admitted **read** fan-out, derived merge, and a **separate labeled debit** into the ledger. Shard primaries do not land portfolio reads on the uniqueness store. Dashed edges are 504 reject, cancel/orphan, a wrongful write-hedge into the authority, and stale partial replies. Recovery: cancel the loser, retry UNKNOWN writes with the **same** key, rebuild dashboards from the fact log, never reconstruct spendable balances from a hedged replica.

<RealExamples title="TokenX hedges reads, never unkeyed debits; ads hedge dashboards, not naked facts">

**TokenX.** Portfolio and token-metadata reads may fan out across shards and hedge a slow primary with a replica after ~p95. Label lag (“replica applied position 240 ms behind leader”). A **debit** uses one `transfer_id` from the client. If the mesh retries or a hedge fires on `POST /debits`, both bodies carry that id; the unique row makes the second attempt a replay of the stored result, not a second spend. If uniqueness is unavailable, **fail closed**—do not hedge to a cache “success.” Speculative execution of the next likely debit is forbidden: that would start money movement the user did not confirm. Cross-AZ hedges add RTT (often 1–2 ms same region, tens of ms cross-region) **and** a second lock waiter on celebrity accounts.

**Ad Click Aggregator.** Operator dashboards may hedge a read of a derived window table on a replica and show “partial: 18/20 partitions, ≤ 15 s stale.” Collectors must **not** hedge an unkeyed POST of billed facts: two appends are two invoices unless `click_id` (or an equivalent unique constraint on the fact log / sink) fences them. Debug click payloads can be sampled or dropped under overload; billed identity cannot. Speculative prefetch of the next dashboard range is a **read** cost; speculative double-produce of a click batch is a billing incident.

<InterviewTraps title="Tail slogans that fail at IC6">

- **“Our mean is 12 ms so the page is fast.”** The page is max of N. Quote parent percentiles, or the math of `1 - 0.99^N`.
- **“We set a client timeout, so the server stopped.”** Timeouts abandon callers. Cancellation stops queries. Count orphans.
- **“Hedged requests are free latency.”** They are extra QPS. Immediate hedges are ~2×. Delayed hedges tax only the tail.
- **“Hedge the debit to the other AZ for availability.”** Without the same `transfer_id` this is dual-write. With it, you still need uniqueness and cancel-the-loser so you do not double lock-wait.
- **“Exactly-once because we hedged.”** Hedging **increases** duplicate attempts. Effect-once is the idempotency boundary, not the hedge.
- **“Partial 200 with zeros for missing shards.”** That understates balances or overstates empty campaigns. Name incompleteness.
- **“Replica hedge is linearizable.”** It is a lagging copy unless you waited for the commit position on that timeline ([Replication and failover](/learn/replication-and-failover)).
- **“Speculative debit of the likely next transfer.”** Speculative **reads** can be cheap. Speculative **money** is an unauthorized mutation.
- **“Fan-out of notifications is the same as request fan-out.”** Inbox copies are write amplification. Max-of-N is wait amplification. Different lessons.

</InterviewTraps>

<Tradeoffs title="What you spend to shrink the tail">

- **Deadline tightness versus completeness:** short budgets cut orphans and p99; more callers see 504 or partial pages. Money still fail-closes rather than returning a guessed debit.
- **Cancel depth versus implementation cost:** socket-only cancel is cheap and leaves lock holders; protocol cancel needs statement timeouts and worker abort but returns capacity.
- **Delayed hedge versus always-on speculate:** delay ≈ p95 buys little extra load; speculate buys p50 improvement at ~2× cost and can **create** a new tail on the dependency.
- **Replica hedge versus primary-only:** replica is faster when the primary is the outlier, and stale by apply lag. Primary-only is current and inherits the primary’s tail.
- **Partial results versus all-or-nothing:** partial keeps the UI useful and can hide a missing liability if you do not label it. All-or-nothing is honest and concentrates failures on the slowest shard.
- **Cross-AZ hedge versus single-AZ:** extra RTT and correlated-failure reduction; still not a durability ACK.

Correctness of TokenX uniqueness and billed `click_id` is not a tail-latency knob. Freshness of a dashboard is.

</Tradeoffs>

<InterviewAnswer>

“I quote parent percentiles, not child means. A page that fans out to N shards waits on the max of N: with independent tails, 1 minus 0.99 to the N is the chance you hitchhike on someone’s p99—about 18% at N equals 20—so the SLO is the parent’s p99, and I budget leftover time per sibling. Deadlines propagate into real cancellation: gRPC cancel, statement_timeout, worker abort. Closing the client socket alone leaves orphaned queries; at 200 missed deadlines per second times 1.5 seconds leftover you are carrying 300 extra in-flight calls. Hedged and speculative copies cut that tail only with a duplicate-load budget—delay to p95 is about 5% extra QPS; immediate hedge is 2×—and both copies share the idempotency key. TokenX POST /debits uses one transfer_id in the same transaction as the uniqueness row; a hedge that mints a new UUID can double-spend, and if uniqueness is down I fail closed. Ads may hedge a dashboard replica with a lag label; they may not double-append billed facts without click_id. Partial results name the missing shard; they never invent a zero balance or a successful debit. Retries of timeouts stay UNKNOWN on the same key. I do not call any of this exactly-once or linearizable replica reads.”

</InterviewAnswer>
