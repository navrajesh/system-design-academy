<BigPicture title="One log of facts; many rebuildable totals">

Prompt: **Design an Ad Click Aggregator** that accepts clicks, deduplicates them, windows them for billing and reporting, survives hot campaigns, and can replay.

The **authoritative billed fact** is an append-only click record with a stable `click_id` (or equivalent) inside a declared uniqueness horizon. Minute and campaign totals, fraud scores, and operator dashboards are **derived**. Serving caches are even further derived.

This is not TokenX. A duplicate click is a billing dispute. A duplicate debit is minted money. Fail **open** enough to keep keyed ingest when a limiter store dies; fail **closed** on uniqueness only where you have a durable unique index. See [graceful degradation](/learn/graceful-degradation), [time-series storage](/learn/time-series-and-append-only-storage), and [stream windows](/learn/stream-processing-and-windows).

Kafka durability for the log, if you use one: RF=3, `acks=all`, `min.insync.replicas=2`, idempotent producer, ISR-only election. Partition key `campaign_id` preserves **append order per partition**, not event-time or causal order ([ordering](/learn/ordering-and-event-keys)).

</BigPicture>

<UnderTheHood title="Ingest, unique, window, then the celebrity campaign">

### Capacity (illustrative)

500 million billed clicks/day, 12× peak around a sports final, 400 B encoded click, RF=3, 50,000 campaigns, one campaign at 8% of traffic.

<Calculation label="Ad Click planning envelope">
Average ingest = 500e6 / 86,400 ≈ 5,790 clicks/s

Peak ingest ≈ 69,500 clicks/s

Raw bytes/day ≈ 500e6 × 400 B ≈ 200 GB; with RF=3 ≈ 600 GB/day before indexes and compression

Hot campaign at 8% of peak ≈ 5,560 clicks/s on one key — size the partition and aggregator, not the mean
</Calculation>

### Path

1. Edge authenticates the pixel or SDK, applies [rate limiting](/learn/rate-limiting) per IP and per campaign (admission, not uniqueness).
2. Ingest API assigns `click_id` if the client did not, records ingestion time, and appends to the log.
3. A uniqueness store (`UNIQUE(click_id)` inside a 7-day horizon, illustrative) rejects duplicates. Horizon must cover the replay window you promise.
4. Stream job assigns **event-time** windows with a watermark and lateness bound. Broker offsets are not business time.
5. Sinks write versioned aggregates keyed by `(campaign_id, window)`. Idempotent upsert on replay.
6. Serving reads **derived** counters with an explicit staleness SLO.

Hot campaigns: do not require every click to hit one lock in the serving DB. Pre-aggregate in the stream job; shard the uniqueness store by `click_id`, not by celebrity campaign, if the campaign key is the hotspot.

<RealExamples title="Sports final, replay, and stolen TokenX pool">

**Hot campaign.** 8% of peak on one `campaign_id` partition overloads a single aggregator task. Split uniqueness by `click_id`; keep a campaign recent-list for serving; do not make the serving row the billed authority.

**Replay after a bad deploy.** Replay the log from a stored offset. Aggregates must upsert by deterministic key. Auto-replay **committed** segments only.

**Shared thread pool with TokenX.** Ads steal ledger workers. That is a bulkhead bug, not “efficiency.”

<Tradeoffs title="Log-first versus counter-first">

**Log + uniqueness + derived windows:** replayable audit; operational lag and watermark policy.

**Direct counter increment:** cheap dashboards; uniqueness and replay become folklore.

**Fail-open ingest:** revenue continuity; overload and duplicate risk if uniqueness is also down.

**Tight watermarks:** faster close; undercount late devices.

</Tradeoffs>

<InterviewAnswer>

“I treat billed clicks as an append-only fact log with a stable click_id and a uniqueness horizon at least as long as replay. At 500 million clicks per day and a 12× peak I plan about 70 thousand events per second and size the hot campaign explicitly.

Ingest admits with rate limits, appends with Kafka acks=all and minISR=2, dedupes by click_id, and computes event-time windows with a watermark and lateness bound. Dashboards are derived and rebuildable. Timeouts retry the same click_id. I fail-open ads admission when the limiter store dies, I never share that pool with TokenX, and I do not call broker order event time.”

</InterviewAnswer>
