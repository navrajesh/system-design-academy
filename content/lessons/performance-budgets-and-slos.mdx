<BigPicture title="An SLO is a budget with an owner, not a dashboard average">

A **service-level indicator (SLI)** is a measured ratio: good events over valid events in a named window. A **service-level objective (SLO)** is the target you are willing to miss some of the time. A **service-level agreement (SLA)** is the contractual consequence of missing a (usually looser) target: credits, penalties, a page to legal. Interviews collapse the three into “we are 99.9%.” A staff+ answer names the event, the window, the percentile, and who spends the miss.

Two SLOs that are not interchangeable:

- **Availability SLO:** fraction of *valid* requests that succeed inside the contract (HTTP 2xx/4xx-as-client, or “billed fact appended”). TokenX fail-closed on the ledger is *allowed* to burn this budget; forging a 200 from cache is not.
- **Latency SLO:** fraction of those successful (or all valid) requests whose end-to-end time is under a threshold, usually a **percentile**, not a mean. TokenX transfer **p99** is a latency budget. Ad Click **ingest availability** is “can the collector still ACK after the billed-fact log,” not “is the 1-minute dashboard fresh.”

Queueing and utilization math live in [Latency, throughput, and queues](/learn/latency-throughput-and-queues). This lesson spends the leftover milliseconds and the leftover error budget. What you shed when the budget is gone is [Graceful degradation](/learn/graceful-degradation). What you scale when the budget is burning is [Autoscaling and capacity planning](/learn/autoscaling-and-capacity-planning). Extra hops and regions spend the same budget: [Fan-out architectures](/learn/fan-out-architectures), [Multi-region design](/learn/multi-region-design), [Networking for system design](/learn/networking-for-system-design).

**Authoritative versus derived:** TokenX spendable money is the ledger plus unique `(tenant, transfer_id)`. Ad Click invoices reconstruct from the **billed-fact log**. Caches, traces, SLO burn charts, and campaign windows are **derived**. An SLO that only measures the cache hop is measuring a photocopy.

</BigPicture>

<UnderTheHood title="Percentiles, hop budgets, error budget, then who pages">

### SLI, SLO, SLA on a named event

Pick the **event** first. TokenX: “client received a transfer response after uniqueness plus ledger commit.” That excludes health checks, admin exports, and analytics produce. Ad Click: “SDK received ACK after the billed-fact batch was on the durable log.” Window sums and unique-user dashboards are out of that SLI.

**Request-based** SLIs (good requests / valid requests) match APIs. **Time-based** SLIs (good minutes / minutes) match “the ledger primary was reachable.” Mixing them hides different incidents: a request-based SLI can hide a quiet 10-minute outage inside a high-QPS afternoon of successes; a time-based SLI can hide a high-QPS brownout inside a still-green minute.

The SLA is looser than the SLO on purpose. If the product SLO is 99.9% and the customer contract is 99.5%, the gap is **internal** budget you can spend on deploys and experiments. Do not promise the SLO in a contract you cannot measure at the same hop.

### Averages hide tails; p50 is not p99 is not p999

Assumptions (illustrative, not a measured TokenX cluster): 2,000 transfer responses/s. In a 60 s window that is 120,000 samples.

- **p50:** half of samples are faster. Planning a pool from p50 under-sizes lock wait.
- **p95:** 1 in 20 is slower. A profile page that fans out to 8 backends, each independently at p95, sees at least one slow child on `1 - 0.95^8 ≈ 34%` of page loads.
- **p99:** 1 in 100. At 2,000/s that is **20 slow transfers every second**.
- **p999:** 1 in 1,000. Two slow transfers per second, often GC, a lock convoy, or a cross-AZ retry. Means still look green.

A mean of 45 ms with p99 of 280 ms and p999 of 1.8 s is a tail problem. Reporting only the mean is how you pass a dashboard review and fail checkout.

<Calculation label="p99 hop budget versus a sync Kafka hop">

TokenX user-visible SLO (illustrative): p99 end-to-end ≤ 300 ms for committed transfers. Availability SLO: 99.9% of valid transfer attempts succeed (commit or a correct 4xx), rolling 30 days.

Hop budgets that must sum **on the sync path** (owners in parentheses):

- Mesh sidecar + LB: 15 ms (platform)
- API parse, auth, business logic: 25 ms (transfer team)
- Derived token-metadata cache: 10 ms on hit (cache owners). Misses must still fit the remainder or fail the cache hop closed for metadata only.
- Uniqueness insert + ledger row: 80 ms p99 including lock wait (ledger owners)
- Serialization and client RTT inside the region: 40 ms ([Networking for system design](/learn/networking-for-system-design))

Sum so far: 15 + 25 + 10 + 80 + 40 = 170 ms. Slack to 300 ms: 130 ms for retries, GC, and one extra mesh hop. That slack is the **budget**, not free space for a new feature.

If analytics `produce()` sits on the **sync** path with a 180 ms hop budget, allocated hop budgets sum to 350 ms and already exceed the 300 ms SLO **before** a real incident. (Summing hop p99s is a **budget** argument, not a statistical claim that path p99 equals the sum of child p99s.) Analytics Kafka is a **derived, asynchronous** hop. It does not get a slice of the 300 ms transfer budget. Ad Click collector ACK **does** include the billed-fact append; dashboard window apply does **not**.

Fan-out: eight parallel metadata reads each budgeted 40 ms p99 still make the parent wait for the slowest child. Hedging and partial results belong in a later tail-latency lesson; here, **do not give eight children 40 ms each and claim the parent still has 40 ms**.

</Calculation>

### Error budget and burn rate

**Error budget** is `1 - SLO` in the same units as the SLI.

Time-based 99.9% over 30 days: allowed unavailability = `0.001 × 30 × 24 × 60 = 43.2 minutes`. That is not “43 minutes the ledger may lose money.” It is 43 minutes of **failed valid events** (timeouts, 5xx, fail-closed rejects). Fail-closed TokenX still burns availability budget; it must **not** burn correctness.

Request-based at 2,000 valid transfers/s, 30-day window: `2,000 /s × 86,400 s/day × 30 days = 5.184 × 10^9` valid events. Budget at 99.9%: `0.001 × 5.184 × 10^9 ≈ 5.18 million` bad events. A 10-minute total outage at full QPS burns `2,000 × 600 = 1.2 million` events ≈ **23% of the monthly budget**.

**Burn rate** is how fast you spend that budget relative to a perfectly even spend. Even spend of a 30-day budget is 1×. If the current error ratio is 1% against a 0.1% allowance, burn rate = `1% / 0.1% = 10×`. At 10× you exhaust a 30-day budget in 3 days.

Page on **multi-window burn**, not on a raw counter:

- Fast window: **2% of budget in 1 hour** ≈ 14× on a 30-day SLO (1 hour is `1/720` of 30 days; `0.02 / (1/720) = 14.4`). Page immediately. Something is on fire.
- Slow window: **10% of budget in 6 hours** ≈ 12× sustained. Page with more evidence; a flap may already have recovered.

Do **not** page on CPU at 70%. CPU is a **lagging** capacity signal ([Autoscaling and capacity planning](/learn/autoscaling-and-capacity-planning)). A JVM can sit at 35% CPU while p99 lock wait burns the 80 ms ledger hop and the error budget.

### Ownership of each hop

A latency budget without an owner is a wish. Write the remaining milliseconds on the ticket:

- **Product / API owner:** user-visible SLO and whether a miss is latency or availability.
- **Mesh / platform:** sidecar, ingress, mTLS. They cannot “take” 80 ms of a 300 ms budget because a new filter is convenient.
- **Ledger / DBA:** lock wait, fsync, connection-pool acquire. This is the TokenX money hop.
- **Cache owners:** hit-path microseconds and **stampede** on miss. Metadata only; not spendable balances.
- **Kafka owners:** produce/consume **if that hop is in the SLI**. For TokenX transfers it is not. For Ad Click **ingest ACK** the billed-fact append **is** in the SLI; window consumers are a separate freshness SLO.

Cross-region: one extra RTT can be 60–80 ms in-region-pair and 150 ms+ across oceans. That hop must be in the budget **before** you draw a second region ([Multi-region design](/learn/multi-region-design)). Fan-out multiplies tail risk, not averages ([Fan-out architectures](/learn/fan-out-architectures)).

### Goodharting: when the SLO becomes the enemy

People optimize the number you page on:

- **Exclude the slow class:** drop “known slow” celebrity-account transfers from the SLI.
- **Success-only latency:** compute p99 only on HTTP 200 so timeouts disappear from the latency SLO (they should burn **availability** or a combined “good-and-fast” SLI).
- **Health-check padding:** 1,000 fast `/health` probes per second drown 2,000 real transfers.
- **Cache-only SLI:** measure gateway-to-cache, never gateway-to-ledger.
- **Dashboard as ingest:** Ad Click “availability” measured as “window table updated in 60 s” while the billed-fact log is falling behind.
- **Mean substitution:** replace p99 with average after a complaint that p99 is “noisy.”
- **Retry amplification hidden:** client retries turn one slow call into three; you count one success and hide two timeouts if the SLI is not attempt-based.

A **good-and-fast** SLI (proportion of valid events that both succeed **and** finish under the threshold) is harder to game than separate charts that you can trade against each other.

<MentalModel title="A monthly fuel tank, hop-sized fuel cans, a speed camera on the tail">

Think of a ferry with a **monthly fuel tank** (error budget). The timetable is the SLO; the ticket refund policy is the SLA. Each engine room—mesh, API, ledger, billed-fact log—gets a **labeled fuel can**. If analytics wants a ride, it brings its **own** tank; it does not siphon the ledger can.

The captain does not watch how hot the engine *feels* (CPU). They watch **how fast the tank is emptying** (burn rate) and **how late the slowest 1 in 100 passengers** is (p99), not the average boarding time. A photographer on the dock (derived dashboard) can be late without delaying the ferry. Forging a “departed” stamp when the vault is still closed is fraud, not availability.

Retain: **name the event, give each sync hop a budget and an owner, page on burn of a percentile SLO, and never let a derived path spend the money hop.**

</MentalModel>

<RealExamples title="TokenX spends a p99 can; Ad Click spends an ingest-availability can">

**TokenX.** Illustrative SLO: 99.9% of valid transfers both **commit** (or correctly reject) **and** finish in 300 ms p99, 30-day request-based window. The uniqueness store and ledger primary are the money hops. Analytics produce, receipt email, and the public price ticker are derived: skip them under [Graceful degradation](/learn/graceful-degradation) when burn rate is high. A celebrity treasury account that holds a row 190 ms does not get excluded from the SLI; it **is** the incident. Adding API replicas because CPU is low is the wrong lever ([Write scaling](/learn/write-scaling) is ownership; this lesson is the budget those owners must fit). Cross-AZ failover that adds 70 ms RTT must be subtracted from slack **before** you call failover “safe”; the durability boundary is still the ledger commit, not the mesh retry.

**Ad Click Aggregator.** Split the SLOs. **Ingest availability:** 99.95% of billed batches ACK after the fact log within 2 s (illustrative). Collectors may linger 20 ms; that linger is inside the 2 s can, not inside TokenX’s 300 ms can. **Freshness SLO** on the 1-minute window is looser (for example 99% of windows closed within 90 s) and **must not** be the number finance uses. If a viral campaign burns ingest budget, sample **debug** fields and pause debug partitions; do not drop billed facts to make a dashboard SLO look healthy. Window lag is derived delay. Lost unlogged clicks are an ingest-availability incident with a durability hole.

<InterviewTraps title="SLO slogans that fail in staff+ interviews">

- **“We are 99.9% available.”** Of what event, in what window, request-based or time-based, excluding which errors?
- **“Average latency is 40 ms so we will hit p99 100 ms.”** Percentiles are not a fixed multiple of the mean. Tails are lock wait, GC, and fan-out.
- **“p50 is the user experience.”** Half the users are slower than p50. Product pain is p95/p99, and fan-out makes it worse.
- **“SLA and SLO are the same number.”** The SLA is the contract. The SLO is stricter so you have budget to spend.
- **“Alert on CPU and disk; SLOs are for quarterly reviews.”** CPU does not spend the user-visible budget. Burn rate does.
- **“If we time out at 280 ms, p99 stays under 300 ms.”** You converted a latency miss into an availability miss—or you dropped it from both if you measure success-only latency.
- **“Exactly-once transfers because the SLO is 99.9%.”** An SLO is not a consistency protocol. Effect-once still needs the unique `(tenant, transfer_id)` boundary.
- **“Multi-region makes failover free.”** Extra RTT and replication lag spend the latency budget. Failover is not linearizable unless you name the consensus boundary.
- **“Dashboard lag means ingest is down.”** Derived windows can lag while the billed-fact log is healthy—or the reverse if you ACK from memory.

</InterviewTraps>

<Tradeoffs title="What you spend when you pick the number">

- **Tight p99 versus cost:** 300 ms p99 needs headroom, shorter batches, and maybe extra AZ-local capacity. A 2 s Ad Click ACK is cheaper and still honest for ingest.
- **Request-based versus time-based:** request-based weights busy hours; a quiet 3 a.m. outage barely burns it. Time-based treats every minute equally and can hide a high-QPS brownout.
- **Separate latency and availability versus good-and-fast:** separate charts are easier to game; combined SLIs are noisier and more honest.
- **Fast burn pages versus alert fatigue:** 2% in 1 hour catches fires and also catches a bad deploy you already reverted. Tune the slow window rather than deleting the fast one.
- **Including retries in the SLI versus counting unique users:** attempt-based catches amplification; user-based hides client storms.
- **Sync derived hops versus async:** putting Kafka on the money path simplifies tracing and couples failure domains.

Do not trade TokenX uniqueness or billed-fact durability for a greener SLO tile. Trade dashboard freshness and debug completeness first.

</Tradeoffs>
