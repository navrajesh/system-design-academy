<BigPicture title="Fan-out copies work; it does not copy the source of truth">

Fan-out is how one event becomes many reader-shaped views: a post into follower inboxes, a TokenX receipt into notification devices, a campaign total into operator dashboards. The **authoritative** objects stay small: the post row, the follow/block graph, the TokenX ledger, the billed-click log. Everything you precompute for a home timeline is **derived**.

Three honest shapes:

- **Fan-out on write (push):** on publish, copy the item into each follower’s inbox. Reads are cheap. Writes explode with follower count.
- **Fan-out on read (pull):** on timeline request, fetch recent items from each followee and merge. Writes stay one insert. Reads explode with follow-graph size.
- **Hybrid:** push to regular followers; skip push for **celebrity / hot keys**; merge a cached celebrity recent-post list at read time.

This is a **news-feed and notification** lesson. Do not model TokenX spendable balances as follower inboxes. A debit has one authoritative ledger row (plus a unique event id). Notifications may fan out; the money must not. Ad Click billed facts stay **one log**; campaign dashboards are a derived fan-out of aggregates. See [scaling read-heavy services](/learn/scaling-read-heavy-services) for cache-as-derived, [partitioning vs sharding vs aggregation](/learn/partitioning-vs-sharding-vs-aggregation) for hot keys, and [ordering and event keys](/learn/ordering-and-event-keys) for per-key order of the post stream.

</BigPicture>

<UnderTheHood title="Push, pull, hybrid, then the races that survive all three">

### Write amplification versus read amplification

**Planning assumptions (illustrative, not a measured product):** 200 original posts/s; mean 400 followers; payload 200 B stored per inbox row; 50,000 home-timeline reads/s; one celebrity account with 40 million followers posts once per 60 s; celebrity skip threshold 10,000 followers.

<Calculation label="Push cost, pull cost, celebrity spike">

Fan-out-on-write at the mean: 200 posts/s × 400 followers = **80,000 inbox writes/s**. Bytes: 80,000 × 200 B = **16 MB/s** of derived inbox appends, before indexes and replication.

Celebrity push with no skip: 40,000,000 copies / 60 s ≈ **667,000 writes/s** for that one post—about **8×** the entire mean write budget—concentrated on one publish.

Fan-out-on-read: each timeline merge that pulls the last 10 posts from 400 followees does **4,000** origin lookups (or cache gets). At 50,000 reads/s that is **200 million** lookups/s unless almost every followee’s recent list is cached. That is read amplification, not “we avoided writes.”

Hybrid: skip push when follower_count ≥ 10,000. The celebrity post is **one** write to a recent-posts cache keyed by author. Regular inboxes stay ~80,000 writes/s. The celebrity tax moves to the **read mixer**: every follower of that author who opens the feed must merge that cache. Cache placement and stampede rules still belong with [read-heavy scaling](/learn/scaling-read-heavy-services).

</Calculation>

Inbox retention is a product bound, not infinite history. Last 800 posts × 200 B ≈ **160 KB** per user. 100 million users ≈ **16 TB** of derived inbox bytes before replicas. When the inbox is full, drop the oldest **derived** row; the authoritative post can still exist.

### Ranking versus materialization

**Materialization** copies “this post is a candidate in this reader’s inbox.” **Ranking** scores candidates at read (or in a nearline job) using recency, affinity, and policy. Do not store a fully ranked feed as if it were the post. A ranker can drop a materialized item; it cannot mint a post the store never committed.

Push materializes membership. Pull materializes nothing until read. Hybrid materializes the cheap edges. Ranking always runs **after** you have a candidate set, and it is derived: rebuildable from posts plus features.

### Pull versus push timelines, pagination, unread

- **Push timeline:** read `inbox(user_id)` in rank or time order. Pagination should use a **cursor** `(sort_key, post_id)`, not an integer offset. Offset `page=3, limit=20` duplicates or skips when a newer row is inserted at the head.
- **Pull timeline:** cursor must be **per followee** (or a merge of those cursors). A single offset across 400 logs is not defined.
- **Unread counters** are derived: increment when a pushed item lands (or when a pull mixer sees a new watermark); decrement on open. They will drift. Repair with a periodic recount from inbox watermarks. Do not treat the counter as a lock on the post.

Delivery of notification copies is at-least-once unless the device sink is idempotent on `notification_id`. That envelope is [Kafka delivery](/learn/kafka-delivery-and-failure), not fan-out math. Consumer lag on a fan-out topic is group progress; see [consumer groups](/learn/consumer-groups-and-rebalancing).

### Backfill is a job with a watermark

When user A follows user B, A’s inbox does **not** instantly contain B’s history. A **backfill worker** copies B’s last N authoritative posts into A’s inbox, advancing a watermark `(followee_id, last_copied_post_seq)`. Concurrent new posts from B may arrive via live fan-out **and** via backfill. Inbox inserts must be **idempotent on post_id**. Completeness is “watermark caught up to B’s current seq,” not “the follow RPC returned.” Until then, A’s feed is **partial by construction**.

A celebrity follow usually **skips** inbox backfill and only records the follow edge so the read mixer includes B’s cache. Instant consistency of 40 million historical copies is not a design; it is a denial-of-service.

### Privacy and unfollow are races

The follow/block graph is authoritative for **who may see**. Inboxes are copies. Sequence:

1. B posts; worker writes the item into A’s inbox.
2. A unfollows B (or B blocks A). The graph updates.
3. The inbox row is still there until a **revoke job** deletes by `(viewer, author)` or the **read path filters** against the current graph.

If you only delete asynchronously, A can still see B’s post from the inbox—a **privacy race**, not a cache glitch you can ignore. If you only filter at read, storage retains copies you promised to forget until TTL. Do both: filter on every read (correctness for privacy), and revoke in the background (storage and unread repair). The post disappearing from A’s feed is **not** instantaneous global delete; it is graph-authoritative hide plus derived cleanup.

Same race for “change post from public to followers-only”: already-pushed rows in non-follower inboxes remain until filter or revoke. Ranking cannot fix a copy that should not have been a candidate.

Overload: bound the fan-out queue. If the worker lags, **shed ranking features and notification copies first**, not post commit. That priority list is [graceful degradation](/learn/graceful-degradation). Do not drop the follow-graph write to “keep the feed available.”

<MentalModel title="A newspaper press, a library query, and a celebrity exception desk">

Fan-out on write is a printing press: one editorial page (the post) is copied onto every subscriber’s porch (the inbox). Cheap to read the porch. Ruinously expensive if one columnist has forty million porches.

Fan-out on read is asking the library to pull every author you follow off the shelf at the door. Cheap to publish. The door becomes the bottleneck.

Hybrid is the press for ordinary columnists and a single display copy for celebrities that the door staff merge in. Backfill is a clerk walking yesterday’s papers onto a new subscriber’s porch, stamping each paper with a page number (watermark). Unfollow is canceling the subscription at the office (the graph) while a paper already on the porch is still readable until someone takes it back.

Retain: **posts and the follow graph are authoritative; inboxes, celebrity caches, rank scores, and unread counters are derived; celebrity keys skip push; backfill has a watermark; unfollow is a privacy race against copies.**

</MentalModel>

**Authoritative state:** the post store (body, visibility, sequence) and the follow/block graph. **Derived:** follower inboxes, celebrity recent-post caches, ranker scores, unread counters, and notification copies. **Consistency boundary:** a post exists when the post row commits; a viewer is allowed to see it when the **current graph** says so at read time. Inbox presence is not permission. Solid edges are commit, regular push, and the read mix. Dashed edges are celebrity skip (reject of push), backfill catch-up (async), unfollow leftover delete (recovery), and unread repair (async). Recovery after a stuck worker is replay from the post log with idempotent `(viewer_id, post_id)` inserts, then advance the backfill watermark; recovery after unfollow is filter-on-read plus revoke, not “the inbox was the truth.”

<RealExamples title="News feed hybrid; TokenX notices; Ad Click dashboards">

**News-feed product.** Regular users: fan-out on write into a per-viewer inbox partitioned by `viewer_id` (hot viewers still skew—see [partitioning](/learn/partitioning-vs-sharding-vs-aggregation)). Celebrities: write one cache list per author, merge at read. Ranking is a separate pass on the candidate union. Pagination uses a cursor. When a user follows a celebrity, skip history backfill; when they follow a regular account, backfill last 50 posts with a watermark.

**TokenX.** A completed transfer may fan out **notifications** (email, push, activity row) to devices on the two wallets. That path can be at-least-once and skipped under overload. The **ledger** is a single debit/credit with a unique `event_id`. Do not write the spendable balance into N follower-shaped copies and call that a feed. Other wallets’ activity UIs may **pull** public receipt metadata; they must not assemble a spendable number from those copies. Cache of “recent public transfers” is derived with a freshness bound; spendable reads stay on the ledger path from [replication and failover](/learn/replication-and-failover) session rules when you need read-your-writes.

**Ad Click Aggregator.** Each billed click appends **once** to the fact log. Fan-out is the **metrics pipeline**: per-campaign counters, dashboards, and anomaly pages. Those views are derived and can lag, sample, or rebuild. Finance still invoices from the billed log, not from a dashboard that was fanned out to 200 operator browsers. A viral campaign is a hot key on the aggregator, not a reason to copy each click into every analyst’s inbox.

<InterviewTraps title="Feed slogans that fail at IC6">

- **“Fan-out on write is always faster.”** Faster **reads**. Writes die on celebrities. Name the skip threshold.
- **“Fan-out on read does not amplify anything.”** It amplifies **reads** by follow-graph degree.
- **“Hybrid means eventual consistency of money.”** Hybrid is a feed tactic. TokenX ledgers are not celebrity inboxes.
- **“Follow returns when backfill finishes.”** That couples an RPC to a 40-million-row copy. Backfill is a watermarked job.
- **“Unfollow deletes the post for everyone immediately.”** Unfollow updates **your** graph edge. Other inboxes, caches, and CDNs still hold copies until filter and revoke. Privacy is read-time graph plus cleanup.
- **“Offset page=2 is enough.”** Inserts at the head shift offsets. Use cursors.
- **“Unread count is source of truth.”** It is a counter you will repair.
- **“Exactly-once inbox insert because the worker is in a consumer group.”** Group assignment is not a sink mutex. Idempotent `(viewer, post_id)` is. See [consumer groups](/learn/consumer-groups-and-rebalancing) and [delivery](/learn/kafka-delivery-and-failure).
- **“Ranking materializes the only copy of the post.”** Ranking can drop candidates; it must not be the post store.
- **“Ad dashboards are fanned-out facts.”** They are derived. The billed log remains one stream.

</InterviewTraps>

<Tradeoffs title="Where you pay: writes, reads, privacy lag, storage">

- **Push versus pull:** push spends write I/O and storage for cheap reads; pull spends merge CPU and origin reads for cheap publishes. Hybrid spends **complexity** (two serving paths, skip policy, mixer) to cut the celebrity tail.
- **Inbox depth versus RAM/disk:** longer inboxes improve offline catch-up and cost storage; short inboxes force pull for history.
- **Filter-on-read versus eager revoke:** filter is the privacy gate and costs a graph lookup on every request; eager revoke shrinks leaked storage and costs a job that will race live reads anyway—run both.
- **Cache of celebrity timelines:** excellent for read-heavy serving; invalidation and stampede are the same problems as the [read-heavy](/learn/scaling-read-heavy-services) lesson. A 30 s stale celebrity cache is usually allowed; a 30 s stale TokenX **balance** is not.
- **Cursor versus offset:** cursors survive inserts; they complicate “jump to page 50.” Feeds should not promise offset pages.
- **Notification fan-out versus ledger fan-out:** notifications may shed; ledgers may not. Ads metrics may lag; billed facts may not.

[Consistency models](/learn/consistency-models) name what a viewer may see (monotonic feed versus read-your-writes on your own post). This lesson only adds: **derived copies make those guarantees harder**, and privacy is a different invariant than freshness.

</Tradeoffs>

<InterviewAnswer>

“I pick fan-out from skew, not from a slogan. Fan-out on write copies each post into follower inboxes: mean 200 posts/s times 400 followers is 80,000 derived writes/s, but a 40-million-follower celebrity is hundreds of thousands of writes per publish, so I skip that key and keep a celebrity recent-post cache mixed at read. Fan-out on read leaves one post write and pays follow-graph degree on every timeline merge. Inboxes, rank scores, and unread counters are derived; posts and the follow graph are authoritative. Backfill after Follow is a job with a sequence watermark and idempotent post_id inserts—Follow 200 is membership, not instant history. Unfollow updates the graph first; I filter every read and revoke leftovers later, because an inbox row is a privacy race, not a delete of the post. Pagination uses cursors, not offsets. TokenX may fan out notifications; the ledger stays one debit with an event id. Ad click billed facts stay one log; dashboards are derived fan-out. If the fan-out queue saturates I shed notifications and ranker features, not the post commit.”

</InterviewAnswer>
