<BigPicture title="Influence is part of the design">

Staff interviews test whether you can move a system **and** a room. This lesson is not a soft-skills appendix. It is how you keep TokenX terminals and Ad Click uniqueness intact when a VP wants speed.

You already have the technical loop in [driving a system design interview](/learn/driving-a-system-design-interview) and the review loop in [architecture review practice](/learn/architecture-review-practice). Here the artifact is language: ADRs, incident notes, and disagreement that still names a mechanism.

Assume: you are the architect for TokenX and the Ad Click Aggregator. Stakeholders include payments legal, ads sales, SRE, and a platform team that owns Kafka.

</BigPicture>

<UnderTheHood title="Four scenes, each with a technical spine">

### 1. Decision record (ADR)

Template you can speak:

- **Context:** peak 4.6k TokenX writes/s; legal wants seven-year audit.
- **Options:** (A) SQL ledger + outbox, (B) Kafka-as-ledger, (C) public chain.
- **Decision:** A. Kafka is integration, not conservation.
- **Consequences:** write availability tracks DB quorum; warehouse is derived.
- **Expiry:** revisit if measured commit p99 exceeds SLO at 2× headroom.

Write it the day of the meeting. An ADR that exists only in Slack is not an ADR.

### 2. Incident narrative

Order: detection, impact on **invariants**, mitigation, fence, customer truth.

Bad: “Kafka lagged so we lost transfers.”
Good: “Outbox age hit 15 minutes; transfers still committed; notifications delayed; no conservation break; we paged the relay owner.”

If money might be wrong, say **UNKNOWN** and stop writes until fenced. Do not soothe.

### 3. Disagreement

A platform lead wants “exactly-once Kafka so we can delete the ledger.”

You: restate their goal (simpler ops). Then: Kafka EOS is a produce-plus-offset envelope, not TokenX conservation ([stream processing](/learn/stream-processing-and-windows)). Offer a joint spike with a Jepsen-style invariant test ([consistency testing](/learn/consistency-testing)). If they still insist, escalate with the ADR options table, not with mockery.

### 4. Strategy

Legal wants regional isolation; sales wants one global balance. Map to mechanisms: regional ledgers + escrow vs one quorum. Cost, latency, and failure are the columns. Pick in writing.

<Calculation label="Disagreement with numbers">
Global serializable commit p99 across 80 ms RTT is not 300 ms transfer SLO without a miracle

Regional primary + async DR: RPO minutes named in the customer contract, not “zero RPO because Kafka”
</Calculation>

</UnderTheHood>

<MentalModel title="Say the invariant in the first sentence">

If your first sentence is a vendor, you already lost the room. If your first sentence is what must never happen, mechanisms have a place to sit.

</MentalModel>

<RealExamples title="Three conversations">

**VP: ship multi-region this quarter.** You present LWW risk with a one-debit-erased story. Offer DR this quarter, active-active next year with escrow. Get a written expiry.

**Incident: ads dashboards empty.** You tell sales the billed log is intact; invoices can rebuild; ETA is the job lag. You do not “refund all clicks.”

**Peer: delete ADRs because they slow us.** You show the Kafka-as-ledger near-miss. Process is cheaper than a conservation incident.

<InterviewTraps title="IC6 behavioral follow-ups">

- Tell me about a time you said no. What was the invariant?
- How do you write an ADR in 15 minutes?
- Incident: who did you page, and what did you tell customers?
- How do you disagree with a principal without escalating too early?
- What metric tells you the strategy is wrong?

Score communication on **precision about state**, not on charisma.

</InterviewTraps>

<Tradeoffs title="Alignment versus accuracy">

**Blunt invariant first:** trust from SRE; friction with sales.

**Comforting slogans:** short meeting; long incident.

**ADRs for every choice:** noise; miss the few that matter (conservation, uniqueness, region).

**No ADRs:** speed; archaeology after the outage.

</Tradeoffs>

<InterviewAnswer>

“I lead with the invariant: TokenX conservation and Ad Click uniqueness. I write short ADRs with options, consequences, and an expiry. In incidents I separate ledger or log truth from dashboard lag, I fail closed when money is UNKNOWN, and I name owners. When I disagree, I restate the other goal, put mechanisms on a table with numbers, and I will not publicly bless exactly-once Kafka as a ledger.”

</InterviewAnswer>
