<BigPicture title="A disaster is a failure domain, not a broker reboot">

High availability in Kafka is surviving the failures the durability envelope named. Disaster recovery is reconstructing a **trustworthy log and a fenced serving epoch** after a failure that envelope did not cover: whole-ISR power loss, an availability-zone or region gone, an operator deleting a topic, retention or compaction eating a fact log, or a restore that would mint TokenX balances from the wrong evidence.

The [delivery envelope](/learn/kafka-delivery-and-failure) is specific: RF=3, `acks=all`, `min.insync.replicas=2` survives **one in-sync broker** (writes pause if ISR falls below two). It does **not** survive correlated disk or power loss of the whole ISR, unclean election, `acks=1`, or history older than retention. [Internals](/learn/kafka-internals) add the physics: ISR is a lag roster, page cache is not fsync, and controller metadata is a different log from partition bytes.

[Backup, restore, and disaster recovery](/learn/backup-restore-and-disaster-recovery) already defined RPO, RTO, committed journals versus UNKNOWN request logs, and fence-before-write. This lesson applies those rules to **Kafka-specific** disasters. It does not re-teach snapshot-plus-WAL for a database, and it does not re-teach dead-letter headers or retry-topic ownership—that protocol lives in [retries, dead letters, and replay](/learn/retries-dead-letters-and-replay). Here the question is: **which bytes still exist, in which cluster, under which identity, and what must not auto-replay.**

Do not claim lossless DR, unbounded exactly-once, or safe failover without naming the copy that survived and the fence that stops split brain.

</BigPicture>

<UnderTheHood title="Name the lost domain, then the remaining copy">

### Broker, ISR, AZ, region

Treat these as different incidents.

**One broker dies.** If it was a follower, ISR shrinks and HW can still advance with remaining members. If it was the leader, the controller elects another **current ISR** member. Acknowledged records that waited for the **current ISR** (at least `min.insync.replicas=2` under `acks=all`) should still be on a remaining ISR member. This is HA, not DR.

**Disk-correlated ISR loss.** Two (or three) replicas share a rack, a SAN, or a power distribution unit. Dirty page-cache batches that producers saw as `acks=all` can vanish together. Internals already said ISR membership is not an fsync certificate. Recovery is restore from whatever copy crossed that domain—tiered storage, a backup, a mirror—or treat the gap as **UNKNOWN**, not as “Kafka will heal offsets.”

**AZ or region loss.** Every replica of a partition that lived in that failure domain is gone. A second cluster, if it existed, is a **different log**. Offsets, producer ids, and group committed offsets are **not portable**. Produce into the surviving cluster is a new append, not a continuation of the old high-watermark.

### Metadata loss versus data-plane loss

KRaft (or ZooKeeper plus a controller) stores **cluster metadata**: topics, partition assignments, ISR snapshots, configs. Partition **segment files** store record bytes.

Restoring the metadata log can bring back names, partition counts, and leader maps. It does **not** resurrect records retention already deleted or compaction already dropped. Offsets in consumer-group metadata without log bytes are not transfers. A recovered controller that points at empty directories is an empty product, not a successful DR.

If metadata is lost and data directories remain, the cluster may not know which replica is leader until metadata is rebuilt from a metadata backup or a documented salvage procedure. Do not invent a leader map from local LEO.

### Operator disasters: delete, short retention, compacting facts

Accidental `delete topic`, a retention.ms cut from seven days to one hour, or `cleanup.policy=compact` on a TokenX fact log are **data-plane disasters that replication will helpfully copy**. Replicas are not backups. The remaining evidence is: a mirror that lagged behind the delete, tiered storage still holding segments, an object-store backup, or nothing.

Offsets without bytes are not money. Consumer lag dashboards that still show a committed offset past deleted segments do not prove the payload exists.

### Poison storms are not regional DR

A flood of corrupt events is a data-quality disaster. The control is the retries lesson: **classify, bound retry, park the key** for TokenX state machines, do not silent-skip. This lesson only adds the blast-radius rule: a poison storm plus “skip so the partition drains” can destroy conservation **without** losing a broker. Page the owner; do not declare a cluster failover.

### Regional failover is a new log

Required properties, then names:

- **Independent durable copy** in the other region (async mirror, dual-produce, or backup).
- **Offset translation** (MirrorMaker-style checkpoint: source topic-partition-offset maps to target offset). Consumers cannot reuse source committed offsets on the target.
- **Cutover plan:** stop or fence source producers, wait for a measured mirror lag (that lag **is** RPO if you fail over now), start producers on the target with **new producer ids**, start consumers from translated offsets or from a sink-known position.
- **Dual-write** (client produces to both) raises the chance both logs have the record and creates two delivery identities; the sink still dedupes on `transfer_id`.

None of this is lossless. Async mirror lag, in-flight acks, and producers that never learned the new bootstrap list are all loss or duplication windows. State them as RPO/RTO, then drill them.

### Restore: copy physics, UNKNOWN produces, new PIDs

Replay from backup or tiered storage **only what was durably copied**. A tier that lags the leader by minutes has an RPO of that lag plus anything never flushed off the ISR. After restore:

- **Auto-replay committed journals:** surviving segment bytes, a mirror that acknowledged the copy, outbox rows that committed with the ledger.
- **UNKNOWN:** in-flight produces (timeout before ack), signed client requests without a matching log record, producer buffers. Reconcile by `transfer_id`. Same rule as the backup lesson; the Kafka instance of UNKNOWN is an ambiguous produce, not a WAL hole in Postgres.
- **Fence** producers and consumers against the old cluster **before** opening writes on the new one.
- **Producer idempotence after rebuild:** a new cluster issues **new producer ids**. Sequence numbers from the dead cluster do not fence duplicates. Business identity (`transfer_id`, `click_id`) still has to live at the sink.

<Calculation label="Illustrative TokenX fact-log RPO and restore floor">

Assumptions (planning, not a measured cluster): 2,000 transfers/s, 800 B/record, 1.6 MB/s encoded produce; seven-day time retention ≈ 1.6 MB/s × 86,400 s/day × 7 days ≈ 968,000 MB ≈ 968 GB unique log bytes. RF=3 copies on brokers are not extra unique history.

Async mirror lag at failover (illustrative): 45 s. Unprotected produce: 1.6 MB/s × 45 s = 72 MB ≈ 90,000 records. That 45 s is the **RPO if you promote the mirror now**. It is not zero.

Tiered-storage copy lag 15 min: 1.6 MB/s × 900 s = 1,440 MB ≈ 1.44 GB not yet off-cluster.

Restore unique bytes into a new cluster at a sustained 250 MB/s: 968,000 MB / 250 MB/s = 3,872 s ≈ 1.08 h transfer floor, **before** RF=3 re-replication, index rebuild, consumer catch-up, ledger invariant checks, and fencing. A 30-minute RTO is incompatible with cold restore of this topic. Warm mirror promotion is an architecture change, not a tighter spreadsheet cell.

Re-replication after restore: two extra copies × 968 GB at 400 MB/s cluster replication ≈ 4,840 s ≈ 1.3 h additional if done serially; parallel replica fetch can overlap but still adds disk and network.

</Calculation>

Game-day: write the RPO and RTO **per topic class** (TokenX facts versus Ad Click dashboards), restore in isolation, validate conservation or billing manifests, then open writes. Prove that rebuilt producers get new PIDs and still cannot double-apply because the ledger unique-indexes `transfer_id`.

</UnderTheHood>

<MentalModel title="Two libraries, two catalog numbers">

A Kafka cluster is a library. The stacks (partition segments) are the books. The card catalog (KRaft metadata) says which shelf a title lives on. Burning one shelf is a broker loss; the copies on other floors (ISR) may still have the book. Burning the whole building is a region loss. Printing a new catalog does not reprint burned books. Compaction is the clerk keeping only the latest edition and pulping the rest.

A disaster-recovery site is a **second library with its own catalog numbers** (offsets). You can truck boxes that already arrived (durably copied segments). You cannot claim the books that were on the delivery truck during the fire (UNKNOWN in-flight produces). You do not let the old librarians keep stamping returns after the new building opens (fence). Sampling a few magazines so the reading room looks busy is **degradation**, not a rebuilt archive.

Retain: **failure domain first; remaining copy second; metadata is not bytes; offsets are per cluster; auto-replay only durable copies; UNKNOWN by transfer_id; fence before write; compacted changelogs are not ledgers.**

</MentalModel>

The source partition log is authoritative for record bytes until retention or compaction. ISR copies are the HA domain, not a cross-region vault. KRaft metadata is a separate authority: restoring it does not recreate expired facts. Tiered storage and backups are recovery sources only for bytes they actually hold. The DR cluster is a **new log** with new offsets. Dashed edges are the **async mirror** (`LOG` → translation → DR), tiered restore, region loss, operator deletion, UNKNOWN produces, compacted-history misuse, and fencing new PIDs—lossy or reject paths, not lossless failover. Compacted changelogs are derived. The ledger is effect authority.

<RealExamples title="TokenX facts versus Ad Click degradation">

**TokenX.** Disputed transfers need the **time-retained fact log** or a backup of those segments. A compacted per-wallet changelog is a derived snapshot: it cannot reconstruct twelve intermediate transfers. After region loss, promote a mirror only up to the last translated offset that copy holds. In-flight settlement produces are UNKNOWN—query clearing or idempotency by `transfer_id`; do not auto-replay producer send logs into the ledger. After a cluster rebuild, producer idempotence starts over (new PIDs); conservation still depends on the ledger unique index. If a poison storm hits during recovery, park the account key rather than skip; that is the retries lesson, not a reason to fail the whole cluster over.

**Ad Click Aggregator.** Losing one **collector partition** (one broker’s leader plus its ISR in the same AZ) loses un-mirrored clicks still only in that ISR. That is an ingest hole with size ≈ produce rate × time-to-restore for that partition. Losing **billed-click facts after retention** is worse: the audit window is gone and sampling live traffic at 1% is **degradation** for dashboards, not DR for invoices. Billing remains closed until a retained fact log or backup can replay to a declared watermark. Campaign config on a compacted topic can be rebuilt from a control plane; billed clicks cannot.

<InterviewTraps title="DR slogans that fail at IC6">

1. **“acks=all minISR=2 RF=3 means we cannot lose acknowledged data.”** It means you survive one in-sync broker, not whole-ISR correlation, not retention, not delete-topic.
2. **“We restored KRaft, so the messages are back.”** Metadata is not the fact log.
3. **“Consumer offsets survived, so we can resume.”** Group checkpoints live in `__consumer_offsets`, not the KRaft metadata log. Offsets without segment bytes typically yield `OffsetOutOfRange` (then `auto.offset.reset`). They are not a backup of transfers.
4. **“Fail over to the DR cluster and keep the same offsets.”** Different log. Translate or start from a sink checkpoint.
5. **“MirrorMaker gives lossless DR.”** It gives an async copy with lag. That lag is RPO. In-flight produces are UNKNOWN.
6. **“Producer idempotence will save us after rebuild.”** New cluster, new PIDs. Sink `transfer_id` is the remaining fence.
7. **“Rebuild TokenX balances from the compacted changelog.”** Intermediates are gone. Use the time-retained fact log or backup.
8. **“Sample 1% of clicks until Kafka is back; billing can wait on sampling.”** Sampling is product degradation. Billed facts need retained bytes or an explicit write-off.
9. **“Skip poison so DR catch-up can finish.”** Catch-up that skips TokenX state machines is silent corruption. Park keys; see the retries lesson.
10. **“RTO starts when the new brokers are up.”** Business RTO includes fence, restore, offset translation, invariant checks, and UNKNOWN reconciliation.

</InterviewTraps>

<Tradeoffs title="Copy lag, restore physics, and split brain">

- **Single-region RF=3 versus cross-AZ ISR.** Cross-AZ raises HA against one AZ and adds replicate latency. It is still not region DR if all AZs share a provider failure mode you care about.
- **Warm mirror versus cold backup.** Mirror shortens RTO and costs continuous network plus offset-translation ops; backup is cheaper and slower and has a larger RPO. Neither is lossless.
- **Dual-produce versus async mirror.** Dual-produce can shrink RPO if both acks succeed and doubles the UNKNOWN surface when only one does. Mirror is simpler for consumers but lag is explicit RPO.
- **Long fact retention versus storage.** TokenX disputes need history; compaction saves money and destroys the DR story for that topic.
- **Aggressive auto-failover versus human gate.** Fast DNS cutover without a fence is split brain. Slow human gate misses RTO. Game-days decide which failures are auto.
- **Rebuild derived aggregations versus serving stale dashboards.** Ad Click can degrade; TokenX ledger cannot serve a compacted guess.

Correctness is the remaining copy plus the sink identity. Availability is how hot that copy was. Cost is how many failure domains you actually paid for.

</Tradeoffs>

<InterviewAnswer>

“I name the failure domain first. RF=3, acks=all, minISR=2 survives one in-sync broker, not whole-ISR disk or power, not a region, not retention, not delete-topic. KRaft restore is metadata, not fact bytes—offsets without segments are not money, and a compacted changelog is not a TokenX ledger. For region loss I promote or restore only what a mirror, tier, or backup durably copied; that copy lag is RPO. The DR cluster is a new log: translate offsets, issue new producer ids, fence old writers before opening produces. In-flight acks are UNKNOWN; I reconcile by transfer_id and auto-replay only committed journals, same rule as the backup lesson. Poison during catch-up parks the key; I do not skip state machines. Ad Click can degrade dashboards; billed facts need retained bytes. Game-days measure those RPO and RTO numbers. I never call that lossless or exactly-once.”

</InterviewAnswer>
