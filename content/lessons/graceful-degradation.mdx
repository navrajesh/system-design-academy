<BigPicture title="Degrade the optional path before the invariant">

Graceful degradation is an explicit policy for **which work you refuse, delay, or serve stale** when a dependency is slow, saturated, or wrong—not a hope that timeouts will sort themselves out. In a Kafka-plus-service-mesh production setting the policy has to name three planes at once: the **mesh sidecar** (admission, timeouts, outlier ejection), the **application bulkheads** (separate pools for HTTP, ledger, notifications, analytics produce), and the **log** (producer buffer, `max.block.ms`, consumer pause, lag).

The ledger, unique-id table, and billed-click fact log are **authoritative**. Dashboards, notification outboxes, debug payloads, and cache copies are **derived**. When Kafka analytics lags or a notification cluster 5xxs, the system should still enforce TokenX uniqueness and still accept billed ad clicks. When the ledger itself is unhealthy, TokenX must **fail closed**: refuse the debit. It must not return success from a stale cache.

Circuit breakers **shed load**. They do not make the sink correct, do not provide exactly-once processing, and do not prevent dual writes. Delivery, offsets, and idempotency stay in [Kafka delivery semantics and failure](/learn/kafka-delivery-and-failure). Retry taxonomy, dead letters, and replay controls belong in [Retries, dead letters, and replay](/learn/retries-dead-letters-and-replay). Queueing math and why unbounded buffers destroy tail latency stay in [Latency, throughput, and queues](/learn/latency-throughput-and-queues). This lesson decides **what you stop doing first**.

</BigPicture>

<UnderTheHood title="Bulkheads, breakers, fallbacks, then Kafka admission">

### Bulkheads: separate wait from wait

A **bulkhead** is a hard cap on concurrency for one class of work so that class cannot consume the whole process. Typical TokenX API isolation:

- HTTP/mesh worker pool: accept and parse requests.
- Ledger pool: unique-id insert plus balance mutation.
- Notification pool: email/push fan-out.
- Analytics produce pool: Kafka producer I/O.

Kafka **consumer lag** is a different queue from the API pool. Isolate **each process** by class: TokenX HTTP must not share the **ledger connection pool** with analytics `send()`. Ad Click consumers must not share **sink** connections or worker permits across billed-click apply and debug-click apply. If one viral campaign hashes to one partition, that partition’s apply loop can pin every shared **consumer** worker—cap **partition-aware permits against the billed sink**, not against TokenX’s ledger pool, unless you have named a monolith that truly shares both. Consumer-group ownership still assigns each partition to one member; isolation here is **thread and connection isolation**, not a second group. See [Consumer groups and rebalancing](/learn/consumer-groups-and-rebalancing) for lag versus high watermark.

### Circuit breakers: open, half-open, then probe

A breaker watches a **named dependency** (RPC, produce, HTTP) for error rate, timeout rate, or consecutive failures in a sliding window. **Closed** sends traffic. **Open** fails fast for a sleep interval. **Half-open** admits a small probe; success closes, failure reopens.

What the open state actually does: it **stops sending more load** into a dependency that is already failing. In-flight calls are not recalled. Records already in a Kafka producer buffer may still flush or time out. Dual writes that already raced still race. The breaker is not a transaction manager.

Name **fail-closed versus fail-open** as a product policy, not as a library default:

- **Fail-closed (TokenX payments, unique-id enforcement):** if the ledger, uniqueness store, or the breaker itself is unavailable, **reject** the debit. Do not apply, do not tell the client the money moved.
- **Fail-open to a degraded path (ads ingest, analytics):** if the analytics cluster or optional Kafka topic is unhealthy, **keep accepting** billed clicks and **sample or skip** derived produce. Availability of ingest wins; completeness of the dashboard loses.

Those two phrases are also used for “what if the sidecar crash-loops.” TokenX payment sidecars should fail closed (no silent pass-through that skips uniqueness). Ad click collectors can fail open to a local sampled disk buffer with a bound, then drain—never unbounded memory.

### Fallbacks: stale, default, skip—never fake a debit

Allowed fallbacks: serve a **stale cache** for TokenX token metadata; return a **default** feature flag; **skip** a non-critical notification; mark a dashboard “delayed.” Forbidden: fallback a TokenX debit to HTTP 200 “success,” mint a new unique id in memory and pretend it committed, or acknowledge a billed click that never hit a durable log.

A fallback is a **derived answer**. State the freshness bound (“balances as of last successful read, up to 30 s stale” is still wrong for spendable money; it is acceptable for a public price ticker).

### Admission control: drop, queue, or degrade by priority

At overload you have three honest moves: **drop** (429, produce exception, sampled skip), **queue in a bound** (mesh queue, producer `buffer.memory`, consumer records in flight), or **degrade** (read-only API, delayed settlement, 10% analytics sample). Unbounded queues convert a 2-second Kafka stall into minutes of stale work; the latency lesson covers Little’s Law and why that happens.

Kafka-specific knobs:

- **Producer buffer** plus **`max.block.ms`:** `send()` blocks until there is buffer space or the wait expires, then the client throws. That is admission, not durability. A full buffer does not mean the broker has the record.
- **Consumer pause:** pause partitions when sink SLO or lag SLO is exceeded. Pause stops **fetch**. You must still **`poll()`** so `max.poll.interval.ms` does not expire; heartbeats alone do not prove a live processing loop (same trap as the consumer-groups lesson). Pause is backpressure, not a drop of already-acked produce, and it does **not** bound broker lag on the paused partitions—producer sample, 429, or `max.block.ms` is what sheds produce.
- **Lag-based admission at the producer edge:** when applied lag exceeds a bound, refuse or sample **low-priority produce** (debug fields, unsampled analytics) before the consumer dies under a growing log.

Retry **budgets** (how many extra calls a dependency may see) belong with the mesh and the retries lesson. This lesson only requires that retries not steal the ledger pool: a 3× retry on notifications must not take the last ledger thread. Cancel work when the caller deadline expires.

### User-visible degradation, named in the contract

Silent 200s with missing side effects are defects. Name the mode:

- **Read-only:** transfers rejected; balances readable from replica or cache with a stated bound.
- **Delayed settlement:** the API persists an **intent** (or enqueues a durable command) and tells the user settlement is deferred. The ledger row is **not** implied.
- **Sampled analytics:** dashboards show a sample and a confidence note; finance still uses the billed-click path.

<Calculation label="Shared pool versus bulkhead under a dead analytics topic">

Ledger concurrency: 2,000 req/s × 0.40 × 0.015 s = 12 busy ledger calls on average.

If analytics produce shares that executor and 1,600 calls/s each block 5 s: 1,600 × 5 = 8,000 threads of wait—far beyond any sane pool. The ledger starves even though its own service time is 15 ms.

Isolated analytics pool of 16 plus a breaker that opens after a 50% timeout rate: in-flight analytics waits cap at 16. Ledger occupancy stays ~12. Transfers continue; dashboards go stale.

Ad Click lag gate (separate example): produce 20,000 debug+click records/s, apply 12,000 records/s, surplus 8,000 records/s. Lag after 15 s ≈ 120,000 records. Pause debug partitions at 100,000 lag (~12.5 s of imbalance) so billed-click apply keeps leftover rate. Sampling debug to 10% cuts surplus by 0.9 × debug share; billed facts stay unsampled.

</Calculation>

<MentalModel title="Ship watertight compartments, then cut non-essential circuits">

Think of a ship with bulkheads: a hole in the analytics hold must not flood the ledger hold. The circuit breaker is the electrician cutting power to a smoking panel so the rest of the ship keeps steering. Cutting power does not put the cargo back in the right crate, and it does not prove a crate was loaded only once.

Admission is the harbormaster: queue a bounded number of barges, turn the rest away, or tell passengers “read-only, no new cargo.” Kafka’s producer buffer is a dock warehouse with a clock (`max.block.ms`). When the warehouse is full, you refuse more boxes; you do not tattoo “delivered” on the box.

Retain: **isolate concurrency, fail closed on money and unique ids, fail open only onto a named degraded derived path, bound every queue, and say out loud what the user lost.**

</MentalModel>

<RealExamples title="TokenX sheds notifications first; Ad Click samples before it drops billed facts">

**TokenX degradation order (highest sacrifice first).** (1) Drop debug and extra telemetry fields on the produce path. (2) Skip notifications (email, push) via an open breaker and a skip fallback—the user may not get a receipt until replay. (3) Skip or heavily sample analytics produce; the dashboard is a degraded derived view. (4) Enter **delayed settlement**: persist an intent, show “processing,” do not move spendable balance until the ledger transaction commits. (5) Enter **read-only**: refuse new debits and credits; serve balances from a replica or cache only if the product labels them non-spendable. **Never shed unique-id enforcement. Never fallback a debit to success.** If the uniqueness store is down, fail closed.

A TokenX debit that already committed must not be retried as a new debit because analytics produce timed out. The analytics event is a separate, at-least-once derived write; duplicates are fenced with the same `event_id` if you do produce after recovery. That pairing is the delivery lesson, not the breaker.

**Ad Click Aggregator.** Keep the **billed-click path** (the records finance will invoice) on the highest admission class: they continue to the durable topic with the usual acks policy while debug partitions pause. (1) Drop debug fields (user-agent dumps, verbose context) so payload bytes fall. (2) **Sample** non-billed or exploratory clicks (illustrative 10%): dashboards lose precision and cannot reconstruct every impression; fraud research loses tail events; you still have billed facts. (3) Only then drop ingest for the lowest class. A viral campaign is a hot key: isolate its consumer permits so it cannot exhaust workers that apply billed partitions. What is lost when you sample: per-click debug reconstruction, exact uniques on the sampled stream, and any model that assumed 100% exploratory events. What is not lost until you shed billed produce: the invoiceable click log.

Mesh outlier ejection can remove a sick analytics pod without opening the ledger circuit. Do not copy the same retry budget onto both clusters.

<InterviewTraps title="Degradation slogans that fail at IC6">

- **“The circuit breaker gives us exactly-once.”** It stops calls. In-flight produces, consumer replays, and dual owners still happen. Idempotency lives at the sink.
- **“If payments are down we return 200 from cache so the app feels available.”** That is a forged debit. TokenX fail-closed.
- **“Fail-open is always more available.”** For ads ingest, yes, onto a sampled path. For unique ids, fail-open skips the invariant.
- **“One thread pool is simpler.”** Inside one process it couples unrelated classes. A hot Ad Click partition becomes a billed-click outage in that consumer; analytics `send()` on the API becomes a TokenX ledger outage. They are not the same pool unless you named a monolith.
- **“The producer buffer is our shock absorber.”** Unbounded `buffer.memory` plus long `max.block.ms` is an unbounded queue with extra steps.
- **“We paused consumers, so those records were dropped.”** Pause stops fetch. Previously acked produces remain in the log until retention. You still poll; heartbeats without poll still trip `max.poll.interval.ms`. Pause does not stop produce, so lag on those partitions can keep growing.
- **“Load shedding unique-id checks under overload.”** Then overload becomes an integrity incident.
- **“Silent sampling.”** If finance or a user cannot tell the dashboard is a sample, you have changed the contract without saying so.

</InterviewTraps>

<Tradeoffs title="What you give up when you keep the invariant">

- **Fail-closed versus fail-open:** closed protects money and uniqueness and costs availability; open protects ingest and costs completeness. Pick per dependency, not per cluster default.
- **Bulkhead count versus operability:** more pools mean more saturation metrics and more ways to mis-size; one pool means a single stuck produce can halt transfers.
- **Drop versus pause versus delay:** drop loses derived events (unless an outbox retries later); pause grows lag and recovery time but keeps the log; delayed settlement needs an intent store and a user-visible state.
- **Stale cache versus error:** cache keeps the page up and can serve a spendable number that is wrong; an error is honest and may storm retries—cap those in the retries lesson.
- **Sampling rate:** 10% analytics is cheap and biased under skew (a hot campaign still dominates the sample unless you stratify); 100% billed facts cost more broker I/O.
- **Mesh retries versus app retries:** two layers can multiply load into a recovering sink. Give one owner a budget; point remaining retry design at [Retries, dead letters, and replay](/learn/retries-dead-letters-and-replay).

Correctness of TokenX balances is not a knob you trade for a prettier availability graph. Completeness of a dashboard is.

</Tradeoffs>

<InterviewAnswer>

“I treat graceful degradation as a priority list, not a timeout. Bulkheads are per process: TokenX isolates the ledger pool from analytics produce and notifications; Ad Click isolates billed-click apply from debug apply so a hot partition cannot take every consumer worker. Circuit breakers open on error or timeout and half-open with probes: they shed load, they do not make the sink exactly-once or prevent dual writes. TokenX payments and unique-id checks fail closed; I will not fallback a debit to success. Ads ingest can fail open onto sampled analytics while the billed-click log stays on the high-priority path—you lose debug reconstruction and unsampled dashboard accuracy, not invoices. Admission is drop, bounded queue, or named degrade: mesh 429s, producer buffer plus max.block.ms, consumer pause of fetch with continued poll. Queues stay bounded. User-visible modes are explicit: read-only, delayed settlement with an intent, sampled dashboards. Retries get a budget so they cannot steal the ledger pool; the retries lesson owns the rest.”

</InterviewAnswer>
