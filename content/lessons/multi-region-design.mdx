<BigPicture title="A region is a failure domain and a legal boundary">

Multi-region design is **where writes are allowed to commit**, **how far copies may lag**, **who may leave the region**, and **how you stop the old region from writing after you evacuate**. It is not “put a load balancer on two continents and call it active/active.”

Two serving shapes:

- **Active/passive:** one **write region** (or one writer per shard). Other regions serve reads from replicas or caches, or sit warm until promotion. Conflict rate on a single object is zero by construction. Failover is promotion plus **fencing**.
- **Active/active:** more than one region accepts writes to the **same keys**. You must name a **conflict rule** (last-writer-wins with a clock you distrust, merge functions, CRDTs, or application reconciliation). Dual-write without that rule is a bug, not a topology.

Locality (read nearby) is a latency and egress win. **Data sovereignty** is a legal constraint: some records **must not leave** a region even if another region is healthier. CAP during a partition is [CAP and practical consistency](/learn/cap-and-consistency-models); session guarantees and bounded staleness are [consistency models](/learn/consistency-models). How you copy bytes and fence a primary is [replication and failover](/learn/replication-and-failover). This lesson adds **geography, DNS, evacuation, and sovereignty** on top of those mechanisms. It does not re-derive Raft.

TokenX **spendable balances** need a **single write region** (or an explicit consensus/CRDT protocol you must actually design). Prefer **ledger authority in one region** plus **regional read caches with a stated stale bound**. Do not fake a multi-master wallet. Ad click **ingest** can be multi-region: per-region billed logs, then aggregate. Dashboards are derived.

</BigPicture>

<UnderTheHood title="Locality, lag, DNS, fencing, then evacuation">

### What “active” actually writes

Per object, list the allowed writer set.

| Object | Allowed writers | Replica / cache | Conflict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TokenX ledger row | One region (or one consensus group you named) | Regional caches, **non-spendable**, stale bound e.g. 5 s | None at the wallet; retries use `event_id` |
| TokenX notifications | Any region | Derived | Duplicates OK if sink idempotent |
| Billed click | The region that observed the click | Cross-region aggregate jobs | Same click id may dual-produce; sink dedupes |
| Campaign dashboard | None (derived) | Many | Rebuild from logs |

Active/active **reads** with a single write region is a common, honest design. Active/active **writes** on a TokenX wallet without a conflict rule produces two spendable balances. Last-write-wins on money is not a conflict rule you want. CRDTs for balances are a research protocol, not a slogan—do not claim them unless you specify merge of debits without double-spend. Prefer one writer.

### Replication lag is RPO if you cut over now

**Planning assumptions (illustrative):** TokenX WAL 15 MB/s in region A; async replica in region B; measured lag 8 s typical, 45 s at p99; DNS TTL 60 s; recursive resolver extra cache commonly 30–120 s; anycast withdraw 2–15 s depending on the provider; in-flight HTTP 5 s client timeout.

<Calculation label="RPO, DNS delay, catch-up, RTO floor">

If you evacuate A **now**, asynchronous replica lag **is** the recovery point: p99 **45 s** of acknowledged-in-A writes may be missing in B. That is RPO for this topology. Synchronous cross-region commit would shrink RPO toward the inter-region round trip (often 60–150 ms one-way transatlantic; **do not** treat that as zero) and add that RTT to every debit.

DNS TTL 60 s does not bound client delay. Resolvers and OS caches ignore or clamp TTLs. **Planning floor:** 60 s TTL + 120 s sticky cache ≈ **3 minutes** of some clients still arriving in A after you flip the record. Anycast can be faster **if** you withdraw the prefix and health checks are honest; it does not fence the database.

Catch-up after A was 120 s behind at 15 MB/s: backlog 1.8 GB. If B can apply 45 MB/s while new writes (if any) continue at 15 MB/s, leftover 30 MB/s → **60 s** ideal catch-up. Promote **after** fencing A, not while A still accepts debits.

RTO is detection + fence + DNS/anycast drain + cache warm + replay of UNKNOWN in-flight, not the 60 s catch-up alone. A 30 s RTO with 60 s TTL and 45 s replica lag is a **contradictory** promise. Tie the numbers to [backup, restore, and DR](/learn/backup-restore-and-disaster-recovery).

</Calculation>

### Session stickiness, read-your-writes, sovereignty

A user who writes in region A and is load-balanced to region B on the next click will not see that write unless you:

- **stick the session** to the write region (cookie, consistent hash that survives failover poorly), or
- carry a **read-position token** `{shard, epoch, commit_position}` and wait or forward to authority, as in [replication and failover](/learn/replication-and-failover), or
- accept **bounded staleness** and label it (portfolio yes; spendable debit no).

Stickiness across regions is not a consistency protocol. If the sticky region dies, the cookie still points at a corpse until TTL and health checks catch up.

**Sovereignty:** if EU wallet PII and ledger rows must not leave `eu-central`, you **cannot** fail those writes over to `us-east` just because latency is better. Evacuation for that partition is **another EU region** or a **read-only** mode. Cross-region replicas of those bytes are a compliance incident even if CAP would allow it. Ads click bodies that include EU user identifiers follow the same rule; aggregates stripped of personal data may leave.

### DNS TTL versus anycast versus the database fence

Control planes people confuse:

- **DNS:** cheap, globally cached, **slow to retract**. Good for coarse region maps, bad as the only fence.
- **Anycast / GSLB health:** faster withdraw if the network actually stops announcing. Still not a WAL fence.
- **Application router:** can check a **region epoch** and refuse writes to a drained region immediately—for clients that already talk to you.
- **Storage fence:** increment epoch / revoke the old primary’s lease so a partitioned A **cannot commit**. This is the actual split-brain control. Timeouts are suspicion; [failure detection and recovery](/learn/failure-detection-and-recovery) is the protocol.

Failover is **not instant**. Even a perfect fence leaves in-flight RPCs, producer buffers, and clients with the old VIP.

### Split brain, fencing, dual-write

If A is partitioned from the coordinator but still reachable by some clients, and you promote B without fencing A, **two write regions** exist. TokenX double-spends. Recovery is not “pick the larger WAL.” It is: **fence first**, accept B’s epoch, treat A’s unfenced suffix as **UNKNOWN**, reconcile by `transfer_id`. See replication/failover; do not restatement-Raft this.

**Active/active dual-write** (client writes A and B) without a conflict rule: both regions may commit different bodies for one id, or one commit and one timeout. The sink must define winners. For ads, dual-produce of the **same click id** into two regional logs is survivable if the aggregator **dedupes**. For TokenX ledgers, dual-write is an integrity bug.

Ads ingest during A’s death: collectors in B keep appending to **B’s log**. Collectors in A that still run may append locally until they die; those bytes exist only if they were mirrored. Do not invent clicks to fill a hole. Delivery and idempotent sinks: [Kafka delivery](/learn/kafka-delivery-and-failure).

Degrade honestly: TokenX read-only in a sovereignty-locked region rather than illegal failover; ads dashboards labeled delayed. That policy list is [graceful degradation](/learn/graceful-degradation).

<MentalModel title="One notary office, branch photocopy rooms, and a slow change-of-address form">

The **write region** is the notary: only that office can stamp a spendable TokenX page. Other regions are photocopy rooms. Photocopies are faster to walk to and may be minutes behind. Putting a second notary on the same page without a merge rule is two legal stamps on different numbers.

DNS is the change-of-address card you mail to the phone book. The phone book’s clerks (resolvers) keep the old address for the TTL they feel like. Anycast is hanging a new sign on the highway; cars already on the off-ramp still arrive at the closed office. Fencing is taking the notary stamp away from the closed office so those cars cannot notarize anything.

Evacuation is locking the doors while people inside are still signing. Their papers are in-flight: you catalog them as UNKNOWN, you do not photocopy a second stamp at the new office and call it lossless.

Retain: **one write authority per spendable key, replicas and caches derived with a lag bound, DNS is not a fence, dual-write needs a conflict rule, failover is fence-then-drain, sovereignty can forbid the healthy region, in-flight work survives the flip.**

</MentalModel>

**Authoritative state:** the TokenX ledger (and unique event ids) in the **write region** that is allowed to own those keys; each region’s **billed-click log** for clicks observed there. **Derived:** regional balance/metadata caches, B’s replica, operator dashboards aggregated from regional logs. **Consistency boundary:** a spendable debit commits only at the fenced ledger in the write region. Replica apply position is freshness, not a second wallet. Dashboard totals are not invoices. Solid edges are admitted reads/writes and RYW/miss forward to authority. Dashed edges are **async** replication, **reject** of a fenced old writer, **DNS cache** after a flip, and **abandoned in-flight** work during evacuation. Recovery is fence A, promote only keys B may legally hold, treat in-flight as UNKNOWN, rebuild caches with a stale bound, and replay ad aggregates from surviving regional logs—not from DNS.

<RealExamples title="TokenX one-writer ledger; ads per-region logs">

**TokenX.** Place the ledger primary in one region per sovereignty class (for example `eu-central` for EU wallets, `us-east` for US wallets). Other regions get **read replicas** and **caches of metadata and non-spendable displays** with an explicit bound (“as of up to 5 s”). A debit from a user whose packet landed in the wrong region **forwards** to the write region or rejects. Do not dual-write the wallet. Notifications can originate anywhere. After loss of the write region, RPO is mirror lag; RTO includes fence, DNS/anycast, and in-flight UNKNOWN. If the only other region is the wrong legal venue, the product goes **read-only** rather than illegal. Session read-your-writes uses a position token, not “the nearest cache.”

**Ad Click Aggregator.** Collectors in each region append to a **regional billed log** (acks policy from the delivery lesson). A viral campaign in APAC does not have to write US disks on the click path. A job copies or aggregates into a global derived store for dashboards with a lag SLO (illustrative 30–120 s). Finance still joins regional logs by `click_id`. Dual-produce during failover is expected; **dedupe** at the aggregator. Do not promote a dashboard counter to an invoice because it was visible in two regions.

<InterviewTraps title="Geography slogans that fail in staff+ interviews">

- **“Active/active means both regions write everything.”** It often means both **serve**. Writes still need an owner or a conflict rule.
- **“Dual-write to two regions so we cannot lose data.”** Without a conflict rule you **create** two histories. Loss window versus fork: pick one and name it.
- **“DNS TTL is 60 s so failover is 60 s.”** Resolver caches, sticky sessions, and in-flight RPCs are longer. DNS is not fencing.
- **“Anycast is instant and safe.”** It can be faster to move packets. The old primary still needs a fence.
- **“We are multi-region so RPO is zero.”** Async replica lag **is** RPO at cutover. Sync cross-region commit is a latency tax, not magic.
- **“Safe failover.”** Failover is fenced promotion plus UNKNOWN in-flight. It is not instant and not lossless.
- **“Read-your-writes because the user hit our global anycast.”** Anycast does not stick a session to the WAL that committed.
- **“CRDT wallets.”** Say how two debits merge without double-spend, or do not say CRDT.
- **“Evacuate, then replay all HTTP logs into the new region.”** That mints transfers that never committed. Replay committed journals only.
- **“CAP says we pick AP for payments during a partition.”** Payments fail closed without a write quorum; see [CAP](/learn/cap-and-consistency-models). Availability of a labeled cache is a different operation.
- **“Move EU data to US for RTO.”** Sovereignty can make the healthy region unusable.

</InterviewTraps>

<Tradeoffs title="Latency, legality, conflict, and recovery time">

- **One write region versus multi-master:** one writer has no merge and a geographic RTT for remote users; multi-master has locality and needs a conflict rule you will operate in anger.
- **Async versus sync replication:** async keeps local debit latency and sets RPO to lag; sync shrinks RPO toward the inter-region RTT and fails or stalls when the link dies (CAP on the write).
- **DNS versus anycast versus app epoch:** DNS is simple and cached; anycast is faster to withdraw and harder to debug; app/storage epochs actually fence.
- **Sticky sessions versus token-forward:** stickiness is operationally easy and wrecks evacuation; tokens port across regions if lineage matches.
- **Regional caches:** they make [read-heavy](/learn/scaling-read-heavy-services) serving cheap and they lie about money if you let them.
- **Per-region ad logs versus one global log:** regional logs keep ingest available under a cross-ocean outage and cost a shuffle/aggregate; a global log orders more keys in one place and concentrates the hot campaign plus sovereignty issues.
- **RTO versus sovereignty:** the legal constraint can dominate the latency graph. Read-only is an allowed outcome.

Link [backup/DR](/learn/backup-restore-and-disaster-recovery) for restore throughput math and [failure detection](/learn/failure-detection-and-recovery) for heartbeat-versus-fence. Do not paste Raft into this answer.

</Tradeoffs>

<InterviewAnswer>

“I treat a region as a failure domain and a legal boundary. TokenX spendable writes have one write region per sovereignty class—not dual-write, not last-write-wins, not a CRDT I did not specify. Other regions get replicas and caches with a stale bound; spendable read-your-writes forwards to authority or waits a position token. Ads ingest is the opposite: per-region billed logs, then derived aggregates, with click_id dedupe if failover dual-produces. Active/active without a conflict rule is a bug. Failover is not instant: async lag is RPO, DNS TTL plus resolver cache plus in-flight RPCs are RTO floors, and anycast still does not fence the old primary. I fence the epoch first, drain, mark in-flight as UNKNOWN, and I will go read-only rather than copy EU money to US. Split brain is two writers, not a health-check failure. Raft details stay in the consensus lesson; here I name the owner, the lag, the fence, and what must not leave the region.”
