<BigPicture title="A live wire is a notification path">

[Fan-out architectures](/learn/fan-out-architectures) already taught push versus pull of **copies**. This lesson owns the **connection**: WebSockets, SSE, long polling, presence heartbeats, reconnect/resume, ordering, sticky L7 affinity, drain of long sockets, and backpressure on slow consumers.

A socket is transport state. Presence (“online”) is **derived ephemeral** state with a TTL. TokenX money does **not** commit because a WebSocket frame was sent. Ad Click live operator dashboards are **derived**; billed append stays on the log.

[TCP, QUIC, and connection lifecycle](/learn/tcp-quic-and-connection-lifecycle) owns HOL and keepalive. [Load balancing](/learn/load-balancing-deep-dive) already said affinity is for connection-bound protocols and still breaks on restart. Use that; do not re-teach Maglev.

</BigPicture>

<UnderTheHood title="Pick a protocol, expire presence, resume honestly">

### Three shapes

- **Long polling:** client GET that holds until an event or timeout. Works through dumb proxies. Occupies a handler per waiter. Simple resume: last event id in the next GET.
- **SSE:** server-to-client event stream over HTTP. Good enough when the client need not send frames. Proxies must not buffer indefinitely.
- **WebSockets:** duplex frames after HTTP upgrade. Needed when both sides chatter (typing, shared cursors). Sticky L7 or a connection-aware router is common; restart still drops the socket.

Do not upgrade TokenX `POST /debits` to a WS message type without the **same** `(tenant, transfer_id)` and ledger transaction. Prefer HTTP/gRPC for commits; WS for “your transfer 409’d / completed” notifications.

### Presence and reconnect

Presence is `last_seen` with expiry (e.g. 45 s after a 15 s ping). It is not linearizable “is online.” A partition shows ghosts until TTL. A reconnect resume token is `{connection_epoch, last_seq}` for a **notification buffer** (derived). Missed sequences mean “gap; fetch from source,” not “the ledger rolled back.”

<Calculation label="Sockets, pings, and fan-out (illustrative)">

1,000,000 concurrent notification sockets, ping every **30 s**. Ping QPS = 1e6 / 30 ≈ **33,000**/s of tiny frames, plus TCP/TLS overhead. At 50,000 events/s of fan-out into those sockets, if each event hits 20 subscribers, egress frames = **1e6/s**—usually impossible; you **batch**, **sample**, or **shard** presence and use the fan-out lesson’s celebrity skip.

Slow consumer: if a client reads 1 event/s and you enqueue 100/s, buffer depth after 10 s is 1,000 events. Bound the buffer; disconnect or coalesce. Unbounded WS buffers are the queueing lesson in costume.

Drain: LB timeout 30 s with p99 session 30 min means you **will** RST live sockets on deploy. Clients must reconnect with resume tokens. That is expected, not an outage of money—if money was not on the socket.

</Calculation>

<MentalModel title="A walkie-talkie, not a notary">

A WebSocket is a walkie-talkie. Presence is “I heard you in the last 45 seconds.” The notary is the HTTP POST with a form number. If the radio dies, you do not sign a second contract; you ask the notary what already happened, then you turn the radio back on.

Retain: **sockets notify, presence expires, resume is a derived buffer, money stays keyed HTTP, dashboards stay labeled derived.**

</MentalModel>

<RealExamples title="TokenX outbox notify; ads operator SSE">

**TokenX.** Transfers stay POST. The app keeps a WS for receipts, device push fallback, and “balance changed” hints. Hints are not spendable; the next GET/replica read is labeled. Reconnect uses last notification seq; a gap triggers `GET /transfers/{id}`.

**Ad Click.** Operator console uses SSE of windowed counts. The stream is behind consumer apply lag. A dropped SSE is not a lost bill. Presence of operators is a TTL for collaborative cursors, nothing in finance.

<InterviewTraps title="Realtime folklore">

- **“WebSockets are faster so they should carry payments.”** Faster notify, not a commit protocol.
- **“SSE is obsolete because WS exists.”** SSE is simpler when duplex is unused.
- **“Presence is truth.”** It is a TTL.
- **“Sticky sessions make reconnect lossless.”** Stickiness dies on restart.
- **“Long polling does not need backpressure.”** Held requests *are* occupancy.
- **“Exactly-once delivery over WS.”** Frames retry; without ids you duplicate notifications—and worse, effects.

</InterviewTraps>

<Tradeoffs title="Duplex and occupancy versus proxy friendliness">

- **WS versus SSE versus long poll:** duplex and efficiency versus proxy and retry simplicity.
- **Sticky versus connectionless GET:** live routing versus easy scale-out.
- **Large resume buffers versus fetch-on-gap:** RAM versus extra reads to the source of truth.
- **Aggressive ping versus NAT idle:** detect death versus 33k pings/s at a million sockets.

</Tradeoffs>

<InterviewAnswer>

“I pick long polling, SSE, or WebSockets from whether I need duplex and how proxies behave—not from a belief that WS is the modern payment protocol. TokenX commits stay keyed HTTP or gRPC; the socket carries receipts from an outbox. Presence is a heartbeat with a TTL, not authorization and not money. Reconnect uses a last_seq into a bounded derived buffer; gaps fetch status from the API. Drain will RST long sessions, so clients must be designed to resume. At a million sockets I budget ping QPS and fan-out amplification from the fan-out lesson, and I disconnect slow consumers instead of growing RAM. Ad Click live dashboards are labeled-stale views of the billed log.”

</InterviewAnswer>
