<BigPicture title="The edge is a photocopy warehouse, not the vault">

[Caching patterns and invalidation](/learn/caching-patterns-and-invalidation) already split CDN, application Redis, and database buffer cache. This lesson owns **edge topology**: points of presence, anycast, cache keys, surrogate keys, purge versus TTL, origin shielding, TLS termination, signed URLs, stale-if-error, and edge compute as **untrusted derived execution**.

A POP HIT never consulted Redis and never consulted the TokenX ledger. Purge is a best-effort rumor across POPs. It is **not** linearizable delete. Origin shield is a coalescing hop that can itself fail.

Spendable TokenX balances and Authorization-personalized receipts do **not** belong on a shared CDN cache. Public campaign creatives and hashed static assets do. Billed click ingest is a **POST to a collector**, never a cacheable GET.

</BigPicture>

<UnderTheHood title="Keys, shields, purge, then what the edge must not see">

### Cache key is the unit of sharing

Default key is scheme+host+path+query, modified by **Vary** (Accept-Encoding, rarely Cookie). Cookie in the key fragments the cache to one user per object and still risks leaking if Vary is wrong. Authorization in a **shared** cache is a cross-tenant incident. TokenX `GET /balances` with a bearer token must be `Cache-Control: private, no-store`.

Immutable assets use content-hashed names (`app.9f3a2.js`) with long TTL. HTML that references them can be short TTL. Surrogate keys let you purge a campaign’s creatives without waiting on TTL.

**Signed URLs** (expiring signatures for object storage) authorize a **download**, not a TokenX debit. Signature expiry is a capability clock, not a ledger fence. See [object storage](/learn/object-storage-and-large-files) for multipart and checksums.

### Origin shield and collapse

Without a shield, 200 POPs on a miss all hit origin. With a shield, POPs miss to one regional shield, which misses to origin. Stampede control is request coalescing: one origin fill per key.

<Calculation label="HIT ratio versus origin (illustrative)">

50,000 asset GETs/s, 95% HIT, mean object 80 KB.

Edge egress: 50,000 × 80 KB = 4.0 GB/s toward users (WAN). Origin without shield: 5% × 50,000 = **2,500** GETs/s. With a shield that collapses duplicate misses, origin might see closer to **unique keys/s**, not 2,500—if 80% of misses are the same hot object, origin could fall to ~500 GETs/s. Cold-cache event: HIT 0%, origin 50,000 GETs/s until filled. That is the stampede [caching](/learn/caching-patterns-and-invalidation) already named; the edge just makes the herd global.

stale-if-error: serve a 60 s stale copy if origin 502s. That is **derived availability**. It is legal for creatives. It is forbidden for spendable balances.

</Calculation>

<InteractiveCalculation mode="cache-load" title="Origin QPS is the miss rate times edge QPS" />

Edge compute (Workers-class) runs **your** JS at the POP. Treat it as an untrusted derived worker: it can sign redirects and strip cookies; it must not be the uniqueness store. A POP is not a Raft group.

</UnderTheHood>

<MentalModel title="Newsstands, not the printing press">

POPs are newsstands. The printing press is origin (and the ledger is not even the press—it is the bank). Recalling an edition is calling every newsstand; some still sell yesterday’s paper until the truck arrives. That delay is purge. A shield is a regional warehouse that collapses orders so the press is not hit by every stand at once.

Retain: **keys define sharing, purge is rumor, shield is coalescing, money is no-store, ingest is not a GET.**

</MentalModel>

**Authoritative:** object-store bytes for public assets (with checksums); TokenX ledger; billed-fact log. **Derived:** POP objects, shield objects, purge queues, edge-worker output, dashboards. Solid edges are HIT/MISS fill and money/ingest paths that skip the shared cache. Dashed edges are purge, forbidden TokenX caching, and derived dashboards. Recovery is hashed immutable names, private no-store on APIs, collectors for POST ingest, and origin capacity for a 0% HIT event.

<RealExamples title="Hashed creatives; TokenX no-store; clicks not GET">

**Ad Click creatives.** Images and video segments live in object storage, hashed, long TTL, HTTPS at the POP. Campaign landing HTML uses short TTL plus surrogate-key purge when legal copy changes. Purge lag of tens of seconds is accepted in the contract. Operator dashboards are **not** this path.

**TokenX.** `api.tokenx.example` is not on the marketing CDN zone, or it is on a separate configuration with `private, no-store` and auth at origin. Edge compute may rate-limit (see [rate limiting](/learn/rate-limiting)) but must fail closed if it cannot reach the ledger—never HIT a cached 200 debit receipt.

**Ingest.** Clicks are POST to collectors. A tracking pixel GET that encodes `click_id` in the URL will be stored at POPs, shared, and prefetch-duplicated. That is how you invent bills or leak ids.

<InterviewTraps title="Edge folklore">

- **“Purge is instant globally.”** It is best-effort per POP.
- **“CDN makes the API multi-region.”** It makes **bytes** multi-region. Writes still need a region story from [multi-region design](/learn/multi-region-design).
- **“Vary: Cookie is personalization.”** It is fragmentation and a leak surface.
- **“Edge compute is a database.”** It is a derived worker.
- **“Signed URL expiry is fencing.”** It is a download capability, not a TokenX epoch.
- **“Hit ratio 99% means origin is safe.”** Cold-cache and purge-all disagree.

</InterviewTraps>

<Tradeoffs title="Freshness, origin safety, and what you must never share">

- **Long TTL plus hash versus short TTL plus purge:** immutable safety versus operational recall.
- **Many POPs versus few:** latency versus origin-fanout unless you shield.
- **TLS at edge versus origin:** CPU offload versus trusting the CDN’s certificate operation.
- **stale-if-error versus fail closed:** derived pages versus money.

</Tradeoffs>

<InterviewAnswer>

“I use the CDN as a warehouse of derived bytes: hashed immutable assets, long TTL, origin shield to collapse misses, anycast for RTT. Cache keys must not include secrets or TokenX Authorization. Purge and stale-if-error are freshness and availability budgets, not linearizable delete. TokenX is private no-store to origin; a POP HIT of a debit receipt would be a forged success. Ad Click creatives may be cached; billed ingest is POST to collectors, never a cacheable GET pixel. Edge compute is untrusted derived code. When origin is cold, I size origin for the miss storm rather than claiming 95% HIT is a capacity plan.”

</InterviewAnswer>
