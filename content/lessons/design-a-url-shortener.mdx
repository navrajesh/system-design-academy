<BigPicture title="A mapping product, not a ledger and not ad billing">

A URL shortener is a **key-value mapping** with a tiny public key, a cached HTTP redirect, and an optional analytics sidecar. The interviewer is testing whether you can quantify a read-heavy path, pick an identifier that survives collision and enumeration, and keep **authoritative mapping** separate from **derived** caches and counters.

Work this prompt:

> **Design a URL shortener** that lets an authenticated caller create a short URL, resolve it with an HTTP redirect, inspect coarse click analytics, and optionally attach a custom alias.

In scope: create, redirect, optional aliases, abuse controls, cache placement, and rebuildable analytics. Out of scope unless the interviewer adds them: TokenX balances or transfers, billed advertising click tracking, arbitrary file hosting, and a full multi-region active-active write story.

TokenX spendable balances are a serializable ledger problem. An Ad Click Aggregator billed `click_id` is a different uniqueness and windowing product. Do not reuse those invariants here. A short code is not a wallet, and a redirect hit is not a billed impression.

### Requirements and invariants

- <code>POST /v1/links</code> creates a mapping from a generated or custom <code>short_code</code> to a destination URL and returns the short URL.
- <code>GET /{'{short_code}'}</code> redirects the browser to the stored destination.
- Analytics answers “about how many redirects in this window?” They may lag and may over-count under retry. They do not authorize creates.
- Custom aliases are optional, first-writer-wins, and subject to reserved-word and lookalike policy.
- Target p99 is 200 ms for create under normal load and 50 ms for a cached redirect at the edge.

The invariant that actually drives the design: **at most one live mapping per <code>short_code</code>**, enforced by a named unique constraint on the mapping store—not by a hash function, a cache SET, or a rate limiter.

</BigPicture>

<UnderTheHood title="Quantify, pick an ID, then place cache around the mapping">

### 1. Estimate the workload

Use this **illustrative** envelope in the interview, and say so: **100 million** stored mappings, peak **10,000 creates/s**, peak **100,000 redirects/s**. Creates at 10k/s are a campaign burst, not a claim that the catalog grows by 864 million rows every day. Average create rate is much lower; the catalog size is the working set you must store, replicate, and cache.

<Calculation label="URL shortener planning envelope">
Catalog: 100,000,000 mappings × 500 B/row (code, URL ≤2 KB truncated in indexes, owner, expiry, timestamps) ≈ 50 GB raw

Three replicas plus secondary indexes ≈ 150–250 GB durable mapping state

Peak create write traffic: 10,000 writes/s × 500 B ≈ 5 MB/s before write-ahead logs and replication

Peak redirect: 100,000 GET/s. Uncached origin egress at 300 B/response ≈ 30 MB/s

At a 95% CDN hit ratio: origin redirect QPS ≈ 5,000/s; a flush or 301-cache stampede exposes all 100,000/s
</Calculation>

Redirects dominate. That is why [CDNs and edge architecture](/learn/cdns-and-edge-architecture) and [caching patterns and invalidation](/learn/caching-patterns-and-invalidation) sit on the read path, while the mapping KV or database remains the only place a create may commit. Partition the mapping by <code>short_code</code>; do not invent a global hotspot. See [partitioning vs sharding vs aggregation](/learn/partitioning-vs-sharding-vs-aggregation).

### 2. Define the API contract

- <code>POST /v1/links</code> with header <code>Idempotency-Key</code> and body <code>{'{destinationUrl, alias?, ttlDays?}'}</code>. Returns <code>{'{shortCode, href}'}</code>.
- Replaying the same caller-scoped idempotency key with the same request hash returns the original code. A different hash is a conflict.
- <code>GET /{'{shortCode}'}</code> returns 302 (default) or 301 with a <code>Location</code> header. Status is a product choice, not a free performance win.
- <code>GET /v1/links/{'{shortCode}'}/stats</code> reads **derived** counters and may be stale.
- Custom alias creates fail with 409 if the unique constraint on <code>short_code</code> already holds a row.

### 3. Generate IDs without pretending hashes are unique

Three common generators, one collision boundary:

- **Hash of the destination** (for example SHA-256 truncated, then base62): same URL can collapse to the same code, which is only correct if the product wants global URL dedupe. Birthday collisions and two tenants wanting different codes for one URL still happen. Insert the candidate; on unique-constraint violation, perturb and retry. The hash is a candidate, not an authority.
- **Sharded counter, then base62:** compact codes (6–7 characters). 62^7 ≈ 3.5×10^12 codes, far above 10^8 mappings. Cost: a counter range is a write hotspot, sequential codes are enumerable for scraping, and you must allocate disjoint ranges per writer. [Partitioning](/learn/partitioning-vs-sharding-vs-aggregation) applies to counter ranges the same way it applies to mapping shards.
- **Snowflake-style** (timestamp + worker id + sequence): no global counter, time-sortable, more bits, clock-skew and worker-id reuse hazards. Still insert under the same unique constraint. Do not claim uniqueness from the timestamp field alone.

Base62 (or base58) is an encoding, not a uniqueness protocol. Whatever generator you pick, the mapping row commit is the moment the code exists.

Creates belong behind [rate limiting](/learn/rate-limiting). Redirects should not share the create limiter key: a viral GET storm is a cache and origin problem, not an excuse to 429 every reader if the mapping is public.

### 5. 301 versus 302, and who may cache

A **301 Moved Permanently** tells browsers and many CDNs they may reuse <code>Location</code> for a long time, often ignoring your wish to move the destination later. Subsequent clicks may never reach your origin, so analytics under-count. A **302 Found** (or 307) is the usual default: you keep the right to change <code>Location</code>, and you can set <code>Cache-Control</code> / CDN TTL yourself (illustrative: 60 s at edge, 10 s in app cache, with jitter).

Caching is still a derived view. A 302 with <code>s-maxage=60</code> is a freshness *budget*, not linearizability with the mapping store. After you update a destination, old Location headers remain until TTL, purge, or versioned cache keys expire. See [caching patterns](/learn/caching-patterns-and-invalidation).

### 6. Analytics are derived, optionally as blobs

Enqueue an at-least-once redirect event after you have a mapping hit. Increment counters in a store that is **not** the mapping authority. If you keep raw hourly slices for later re-aggregation, write immutable objects—see [object storage and large files](/learn/object-storage-and-large-files)—and treat them as rebuildable input, not as the live redirect path. Do not import Ad Click windowing or billed uniqueness into this design unless the interviewer changes the product.

<MentalModel title="Tiny public key, fat private record, photocopied Location">

Think of a coat-check token. The plastic number is public and short. The coat, owner, and expiry live behind the counter. Photocopying the number onto a sticky note on the door (CDN) makes lookup fast, but the sticky note is not the logbook. If someone reprints a number, the logbook's unique index—not the photocopy—rejects it.

Retain this loop:

1. **Requirement:** create, redirect, optional alias, approximate counts.
2. **Constraint:** 100M mappings, 10k writes/s peak, 100k redirects/s peak, abuse, hot viral keys.
3. **Decision:** unique mapping store; compact ID with insert-retry; 302 plus explicit edge TTL; rate-limit creates.
4. **Consequence:** cache and counters can be wrong or missing; creates fail when the store cannot prove uniqueness.

</MentalModel>

The mapping store owns <code>short_code → destination</code> in one consistency boundary. Unique constraints on <code>short_code</code> and on <code>(caller_id, idempotency_key)</code> are inside that boundary. CDN, origin shield, and app cache hold **derived** Location photocopies. Click counters and hourly blobs are a second, asynchronous, at-least-once boundary. Solid edges are the create commit and the cache-fill read. Dashed edges are failure or async paths: unique-constraint / store-down back to writes, cache miss toward origin, mapping unavailability on redirect, and analytics enqueue.

<RealExamples title="Create, viral redirect, and a destination change">

### Successful create

A caller posts <code>https://example.com/launch</code> with an idempotency key. The write path rate-limits, scans the destination, allocates a 7-character base62 code from a sharded counter, and inserts the mapping. The unique index accepts the row. The API returns <code>https://short.example/aB3dE9x</code>. A retry with the same key returns that code; it does not allocate a second one.

### Viral hot key

A celebrity posts the same short URL. Redirect QPS on one <code>short_code</code> jumps from tens to tens of thousands. Hash sharding of the catalog does not split one key. The CDN must hold that Location. On miss, singleflight one origin fill. A dedicated hot-key replica in the app cache is reasonable. Do not add 1,000 mapping shards to fix one celebrity code.

### Destination update versus 301

The owner points the code at a new landing page. If you issued **301** with a long browser cache, many clients never ask again. If you issued **302** with a 60 s edge TTL, the wrong Location lasts until expiry or purge. Either way, say the staleness window. Do not claim “invalidate the CDN” is linearizable with every phone's HTTP cache.

<InterviewTraps title="Staff+ follow-ups for a URL shortener">

### Questions the interviewer should be able to ask

- Where does a short code become unique, and what happens on hash collision?
- Why not treat a Redis SET as the mapping authority?
- 301 or 302, and what happens to analytics and destination edits?
- What is the origin QPS at 100k edge redirects and a 5% miss ratio, including a flush?
- How do you stop sequential codes from being scraped, and aliases from impersonating brands?
- What fails when the cache dies versus when the mapping store dies?
- How do you handle one viral key without recasting the whole catalog?
- Are click counts exact, and how is that different from billed ad clicks?

### Staff+ scoring rubric

- **Requirements:** create, redirect, optional alias, derived analytics; explicitly excludes TokenX balances and Ad Click billing.
- **Quantification:** 100M mappings, 10k writes/s, 100k redirects/s, row size, replica storage, origin QPS after hit ratio—all with units.
- **Correctness:** unique constraint on <code>short_code</code>, idempotency unique constraint, insert-retry for generators, no exactly-once claim on analytics.
- **Architecture:** mapping store authoritative; CDN/app cache and counters derived; dashed failure and async edges named.
- **Operations:** abuse, enumeration, hot keys, cache stampede, store loss, queue lag, purge versus TTL.
- **Evolution:** delay extra mapping shards until measured; isolate hot keys with cache, not with wishful partition counts.
- **Communication:** 301/302 is a product trade, not a slogan; cache freshness is a budget.

A weak answer says “hash the URL, put it in Redis, 301 everything, Kafka gives exact counts.” A defensible answer names the unique index, the cache as a photocopy, and the store-versus-cache failure split.

</InterviewTraps>

<Tradeoffs title="Why this design—and when it stops fitting">

**Unique mapping in KV or a relational store:** one collision story and a simple redirect miss fill, but create availability tracks store quorum or primary health.

**Hash IDs:** good when global URL dedupe is desired; bad when two owners need two codes, and still requires insert-retry.

**Sharded counters plus base62:** shortest codes and easy bulk allocation; enumerable IDs and counter-range hotspots unless ranges are partitioned.

**Snowflake-style IDs:** no global counter; longer codes, worker-id ops, and clock assumptions.

**302 plus short edge TTL:** destination edits and origin analytics remain possible; origin QPS and CDN cost stay higher than a hard 301.

**301 plus long browser cache:** lowest origin load; you lose destination agility and most subsequent click signals.

**Derived click counters and hourly blobs:** cheap to scale and rebuild; duplicates and lag are expected. Promoting them to mapping authority would be a correctness bug.

This design becomes inappropriate when the product needs globally unique billed events (that is an Ad Click Aggregator), spendable balances (TokenX), or immediate worldwide destination updates with no stale Location. Those require different authorities, not a smarter TTL.

</Tradeoffs>

<InterviewAnswer>

“I scope a URL shortener to create mappings, HTTP redirects, optional custom aliases, and derived analytics—not TokenX balances and not billed ad clicks.

Illustrative envelope: 100 million stored mappings, 10 thousand peak creates per second, 100 thousand peak redirects per second. At about 500 bytes a row, the catalog is tens of gigabytes before three replicas and indexes. Redirects are the hot path.

The mapping KV or database is authoritative. A unique constraint on short_code is the collision boundary; a second unique constraint on caller plus idempotency key stops retry from minting extra codes. Hash IDs insert and retry. Sharded counters give compact base62 codes but are enumerable. Snowflake-style IDs avoid a global counter and still need that unique index. Creates are rate-limited and abuse-scanned.

Redirects read derived CDN and app caches. I default to 302 with an explicit short TTL so destinations can change and origin still sees many clicks. 301 plus a long TTL is a product choice that freezes Location. Cache loss raises origin QPS; mapping-store loss fails creates and leaves only leftover cache for GET. Viral codes need singleflight, not extra shards. Click counters and hourly blobs are at-least-once derivatives and never the mapping authority.”

</InterviewAnswer>
