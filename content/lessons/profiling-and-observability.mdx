<BigPicture title="Telemetry is a derived view of a running system">

Observability answers **why this request, disk, or core is slow or wrong**. It does not create durability. A trace ID in a header is not an idempotency key unless you stored it in the uniqueness table. A dashboard that plots “exactly the billed clicks” is still a **derived** time series until finance can replay identities from the fact log.

Four signals, four jobs:

- **Metrics:** cheap, aggregated, high-frequency. Good for [performance budgets and SLOs](/learn/performance-budgets-and-slos) and for queueing from [latency, throughput, and queues](/learn/latency-throughput-and-queues).
- **Traces:** a sampled graph of spans across services. Good for fan-out and p99 **exemplars**. Not a linearizable history of money.
- **Logs:** high-cardinality narrative. Put **debug** attributes here, not as metric labels. Put **billed identity** in the append-only fact log, not only in a log line that expires in 14 days.
- **Profiles:** stack samples over time (continuous profiling). Good for CPU, allocation, and lock wait **inside one process**. Not a substitute for RED on the API or USE on the disk.

Cross-lesson spine: time-series cardinality and retention live in [time-series and append-only storage](/learn/time-series-and-append-only-storage); Kafka page-cache and lag are [Kafka internals](/learn/kafka-internals); chaos and recovery drills are [disaster scenarios](/learn/disaster-scenarios); proving a consistency claim is [consistency testing](/learn/consistency-testing). This lesson is how you **instrument** those boundaries without lying with averages.

</BigPicture>

<UnderTheHood title="RED versus USE, four telemetry types, then the failure modes">

### RED for requests, USE for resources

**RED** (rate, errors, duration) is for **work the user asked for**: TokenX `POST /transfers`, Ad Click collector ingest, a consumer’s apply loop treated as a request.

- **Rate:** offered and completed operations per second (name which).
- **Errors:** failed operations per second, by **cause class** (4xx fence, 5xx dependency, timeout), not by every `campaign_id`.
- **Duration:** histogram of latency with percentiles. p50 is not the SLO; p99 and an **exemplar** are.

**USE** (utilization, saturation, errors) is for **resources that do the work**: CPU, disk, NIC, connection pool, Kafka partition, ledger row lock.

- **Utilization:** busy time / capacity (CPU 70%, disk 40% write, pool 26/50 busy).
- **Saturation:** queue length or wait: run-queue, disk wait, pool waiters, consumer lag, lock wait.
- **Errors:** hardware and resource faults (disk EIO, NIC drops), distinct from RED application errors.

A TokenX API at RED rate 2,000 /s, error 0.1%, duration p99 180 ms can still be dying: USE on the uniqueness pool is 95% utilized with 40 waiters. CPU RED looks fine; the resource is saturated. That is the same lesson as pool math in [latency, throughput, and queues](/learn/latency-throughput-and-queues), measured instead of guessed.

### Traces, metrics, logs, profiles

A **metric** is an aggregated number with a small label set. A **trace** is a tree of timed spans sharing a `trace_id`. A **log** is an event with arbitrary fields. A **profile** is a statistical stack (CPU, wall, alloc, lock) over a window.

Use them together: RED metrics trip the burn; an **exemplar** attaches a `trace_id` that actually landed in the p99 bucket; the trace shows the span that waited on the ledger; the profile on that host shows the mutex or JSON codec; the log (or fact log) holds the `transfer_id` / `click_id` you can audit.

<Callout type="warning" title="Tracing is not linearizability">

A trace is a **best-effort, sampled, clock-skewed** recording of spans. Clock skew of tens of milliseconds between hosts is normal. Missing spans are normal under sampling. Span order in a UI is not a proof that debit A happened-before debit B in the ledger. Linearizability and session guarantees are properties of the **store and the protocol**, proven with [consistency testing](/learn/consistency-testing), not with a waterfall chart.

</Callout>

### Exemplars, sampling bias, cardinality

**Head sampling** (keep 1% of traces at the edge) **under-represents the tail**. The p99 you bill an SLO on is exactly the 1% you are most likely to drop. Prefer **tail sampling** (keep errors and slow traces) plus a small head sample for baseline, or attach **exemplars**: when a histogram bucket increments, keep one trace id for that bucket.

**Cardinality explosion:** each unique combination of metric labels is a time series. `http_requests_total{path, code}` is fine. `http_requests_total{campaign_id}` with 200,000 campaigns is 200,000 series **per scrape dimension**, and it will take down the TSDB long before it helps billing. Put `campaign_id` in a **log** or in the **billed-fact log**. Debug identity is high-cardinality by nature; billed identity is **authoritative append-only**, not a Prometheus label.

Planning assumptions (illustrative, not a measured cluster): TokenX 2,000 transfers/s; Ad Click 80,000 clicks/s peak; scrape interval 15 s; 4 RED series per RPC; 200,000 active campaigns.

<Calculation label="Cardinality versus sampled traces (illustrative)">

Ad Click RED without campaign: 12 RPCs × 4 series × 3 pods = 144 series. Cheap.

With `campaign_id` label, keep the same pod factor: 200,000 campaigns × 12 RPCs × 4 series × 3 pods = **28.8 million** series. At a 15 s scrape that is 28.8×10^6 / 15 ≈ **1.92 million samples/s** of **useless** identity. The billed-fact log at 80,000 × 400 B = 32 MB/s peak is the correct identity store.

Head-sample 1% of 2,000 TokenX transfers/s → 20 traces/s exported. About 1% of transfers are slower than p99, so ~20 /s sit in the tail; expected **complete tail traces** kept ≈ 20 × 0.01 = **0.2 /s** (~12 per minute)—sparse and biased, not “often zero.” Histogram **exemplars** can still attach a `trace_id` to the p99 bucket even when full traces are missing. Head sampling drops exported traces; it does not delete the histogram. Tail sampling that keeps duration ≥ p99 keeps those ~20 /s complete traces.

Trace ID as fence: 2,000 retries/s with only `trace_id` and no `(tenant, transfer_id)` unique row double-applies on retry. Observability did not store the fence.

</Calculation>

<InteractiveCalculation mode="little-law" title="In-flight requests versus what a 1% head sample will see" />

Little’s Law still bounds how many requests exist to observe. At 2,000 /s and 200 ms, L ≈ 400 in flight. A 1% sample sees about 4 of those 400 at any instant—and is biased to the fast ones if sampling happens at admission rather than at completion.

<MentalModel title="The black box is not the bank vault">

A flight recorder (traces, profiles, RED/USE) explains why the plane shook. The **bank vault ledger** is still the paper that says who owns the cash. CCTV with a timestamp (a log line, a span) is useful in court only if you also have the **numbered receipt** stored in the uniqueness table or the billed-fact log.

Head sampling is a security camera that turns off during the robbery because “1% of minutes is enough.” Cardinality is labeling every grain of rice so the pantry inventory system dies. Continuous profiling is a stethoscope on one engine; it does not replace airspeed (RED) or fuel-tank gauges (USE).

Retain: **RED for work, USE for resources, profiles for stacks, exemplars for the tail, logs for high-cardinality debug, fact logs and unique rows for identity—and never confuse a trace with a commit.**

</MentalModel>

<RealExamples title="TokenX traces a transfer; Ad Click refuses campaign_id labels">

**TokenX.** Propagate `trace_id` on the transfer API for support and p99 exemplars. The **fence** remains `(tenant, transfer_id)` in the same ledger transaction as the debit. A support engineer opens trace T-77 to see 240 ms lock wait; finance still reconstructs the transfer from the ledger, not from Jaeger. Outbox/CDC to analytics is asynchronous; a missing analytics span does not unwind money. If you need to prove isolation or ordering, you write a [consistency testing](/learn/consistency-testing) harness against the store, not a screenshot of a waterfall. Disaster drills in [disaster scenarios](/learn/disaster-scenarios) should assert **ledger recovery**, then confirm that traces for the outage window are **best-effort** (retention 7–30 days illustrative)—the vault outlives the recorder.

**Ad Click Aggregator.** Collectors export RED: ingest rate, error class, batch duration. Brokers and disks get USE: disk utilization, replica fetch saturation, ISR shrink as an **error** on the resource. Continuous profiling on the aggregator finds a hot JSON path. **Do not** put `campaign_id` on the ingest counter. Viral campaign debug uses a **log** sample or a query against the billed-fact log. Windowed dashboard series are derived rollups ([time-series and append-only storage](/learn/time-series-and-append-only-storage)). If metrics and the fact log disagree, the **fact log wins** for invoices; metrics are wrong or lagged.

<InterviewTraps title="Observability slogans that fail in staff+ interviews">

- **“We have tracing, so we are linearizable.”** Tracing does not order commits. Clocks skew. Spans drop.
- **“Metrics are lossless identity.”** Aggregates lose keys. Labels at campaign cardinality melt the TSDB.
- **“p99 is fine; average latency is 12 ms.”** The tail is the SLO. Head sampling hides it.
- **“USE is the same as RED.”** RED is work; USE is the engine. CPU 35% with a saturated pool is a USE finding.
- **“Continuous profiling replaces APM.”** Profiles lack cross-service context and request identity.
- **“The trace ID is our idempotency key.”** Only if it is stored in the unique table—and you should store the **product** id, because traces churn on retry.
- **“Logs are the billed-click log.”** Application logs are retention- and sampling-limited debug. Finance needs the append-only fact log.
- **“Observability gives durability.”** A span in memory is not an fsync. Crash the collector and the un-ACKed batch is gone regardless of OpenTelemetry.
- **“Exactly-once delivery because we trace every produce.”** Delivery is at-least-once plus an idempotent sink. The trace is not the sink.

</InterviewTraps>

<Tradeoffs title="What you spend to see the tail without lying">

- **Head versus tail sampling:** head is cheap and biased; tail keeps the SLO cases and costs more backend ingest.
- **Exemplars versus full traces:** exemplars are a few ids per bucket; full traces explain one request end to end but need a budget.
- **Metric labels versus logs versus fact log:** labels must stay low-cardinality; logs hold debug keys; facts hold invoice identity and cost storage.
- **RED versus USE versus profiles:** you need all three at different layers; one graph cannot replace the others.
- **Telemetry retention versus ledger/fact retention:** traces (illustrative 7–30 days) are cheaper to drop than WAL or billed facts.
- **Scrape interval versus cost and lag:** 15 s is operationally common; 1 s multiplies TSDB write and still is not a commit log.

Correctness of TokenX balances and billed-click identity is not a telemetry knob. Completeness of a debug view is.

</Tradeoffs>

<InterviewAnswer>

“I split signals: RED for user work—rate, errors, duration histograms—and USE for resources—utilization, saturation, errors on CPU, pools, disks, partitions. Profiles tell me which stacks burn CPU or hold locks on one box. Traces explain one request; they are sampled and clock-skewed, so I do not call them linearizable and I do not use a trace id as a TokenX fence. The fence is (tenant, transfer_id) in the uniqueness table with the debit. For p99 I attach exemplars and I tail-sample errors and slow traces, because a 1% head sample hides the SLO. High-cardinality fields like campaign_id go in logs or the billed-fact log, never as metric labels, or the TSDB dies and still cannot invoice. Ads: ACK after the fact log; dashboards are derived. Observability is a derived view. It does not fsync money, it does not replace consistency tests, and if traces and the ledger disagree, the ledger wins.”

</InterviewAnswer>
