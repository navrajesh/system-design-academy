<NonNegotiable title="Produce durability is not sink commit">

A durable log stores **records until retention**. It does not store TokenX balances. Produce acknowledgement is “this offset is on the replica set you waited for.” Sink commit is a second boundary. Consumer offsets are **group progress**, not proof the sink applied, and not a settlement record.

State the assumptions:

- **Consistency:** append order is per partition (or per shard), not event time, not causal order, not a linearizable money history. At-least-once delivery to external sinks unless you name a produce-plus-offset transaction **and** an idempotent sink.
- **Durability:** Kafka-class planning envelope is RF=3, `acks=all` (every replica currently in the ISR), `min.insync.replicas=2`, producer idempotence, ISR-only election. That survives one in-sync broker loss. It does not survive correlated ISR disk loss, `acks=1`, unclean election, or history older than retention. Managed shard logs last for the retention you pay for. An outbox row lasts as long as the primary’s WAL/backup story.
- **Ordering:** one partition key, one producer waiting for acks, gives broker append order for that key. Independent keys interleave. Windows and watermarks are **job policy** on the consumer.
- **Availability:** `minISR=2` **rejects** writes when the ISR shrinks too far. Managed shards throttle. An outbox poller crash delays fan-out; the ledger row remains. You cannot promote a lagging consumer as leader of money.
- **Failure:** dual-write (commit money, then produce) can lose either side. Offset-before-apply loses work. Apply-before-offset repeats work. A dead-letter topic is another log with an owner, not a trash can.

Money stays on the ledger. Kafka, shard logs, and outbox copies are derived integration **after** that commit, except where the product’s authoritative fact **is** the billed append (Ad Click `click_id`).

</NonNegotiable>

<Options title="Three log (or no-log) classes">

Defend one. Pulsar-class segmented logs sit on the same landscape matrix as a sibling of Kafka-class; they are not a fourth interview default here.

1. **Partitioned log (Kafka-class).** Durable append-only facts, independent consumer groups, replay inside retention, partition-key order. Use after a TokenX outbox, or as the Ad Click billed-fact log once `click_id` is unique. Offsets are not ledger proof. Hot keys pin one partition. Adding partitions is a key-mapping change.
2. **Managed shard log (Kinesis-class).** AWS-centric ingest, partition keys, you do not SSH the broker. Per-shard sequence order. Replay dies when paid retention dies (24 h unless extended). Iterator position is progress, not a SQL commit. Throughput is planned per shard, not “infinite because it is managed.”
3. **Database outbox-only.** The primary already commits the business row **and** the outbox row in one transaction. A few workers poll or CDC-apply at-least-once to idempotent sinks. No independent multi-reader log, no days-long replay across many groups. Dual-write without an outbox is not this option.

<InvalidatingFailure title="Offset or request log as the settlement record">

The popular option is “the consumer committed offset 1000, so those transfers settled” or “we have the signed request log, replay it into the ledger after restore.” Both fail.

An offset is where a group will poll next. A crash after sink commit and before offset commit **replays**. A crash after offset commit and before sink commit **skips**. Neither event is `CREDITED` or `CANCELLED`. Replaying a request log after restore can mint a second debit: the request was never a journal. TokenX restore replays **committed journals**, then fences old epochs. UNKNOWN client timeouts retry the same tenant-scoped key against uniqueness, not against a topic end offset.

Ad Click may rebuild windows from the billed-fact log by `click_id`. That still does not make a dashboard total an invoice, and it does not make a TokenX request log a ledger.

</InvalidatingFailure>

<OperationalBurden title="What the interview answer must staff">

- **Kafka-class:** brokers, disks, ISR, controller/KRaft, retention, consumer groups, DLQ owner and age SLO. Replication bytes are roughly produce times RF. You name `acks`, `minISR`, and election policy in the interview, not “Kafka is durable.”
- **Managed shards:** shard-hour or on-demand cost, PUT and extended retention, iterator expiry, no on-disk hexdump when you need to prove a record. Regional impairment is the vendor’s story plus your UNKNOWN retries.
- **Outbox-only:** publisher or CDC slot, lag into the primary, idempotent sinks, and backpressure so the slot does not halt WAL. Cheaper than a cluster until you need many independent replayable consumers.

TokenX ACK still waits on the ledger transaction, not on the poller or the produce.

</OperationalBurden>
