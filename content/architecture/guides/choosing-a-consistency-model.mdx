<NonNegotiable title="Name the history, not a two-letter sticker">

CAP is a per-operation policy under a named partition, not a permanent C/A/P badge on a product. Linearizability is a protocol (majority commit plus a read that proves current term), not a default of PostgreSQL `READ COMMITTED`, DynamoDB eventual indexes, or Kafka.

State the assumptions:

- **Consistency:** a consensus log can offer one history for that group when writes commit on a current-term majority and reads use ReadIndex or a bounded-clock lease plus apply wait. Dynamo-style `W+R>N` is overlap under stable membership, not linearizability; sloppy quorums, hints, and concurrent versions need a named repair. CRDTs converge by merge; last-write-wins discards siblings on a timestamp you must trust. Convergence is not business validity.
- **Durability:** consensus durability is “majority fsync of that command.” Quorum durability is “the W replicas you waited for,” which at `W=ONE` is a single node. CRDT/LWW durability is whatever each replica flushed before the next crash.
- **Ordering:** consensus is one log order per group. Quorum stores do not pick a single order until repair. CRDT merge is associative and commutative; LWW order is a clock tuple, not physics.
- **Availability:** `W=ONE` stays writable when a majority of peers are unreachable, and **can lose the copy that had the write**. `W=ALL` waits for every replica and goes unavailable on one loss, in exchange for not acknowledging a write that missed a copy. Consensus minority partitions **cannot commit**; that is the TokenX debit policy, not “the cluster is down.”
- **Failure:** leader crash needs a new term and fencing. Isolated minority must not become a second ledger. Clock skew rewrites LWW winners. A health-check VIP that removes dead sockets is **not** consensus.

DynamoDB `ConsistentRead` is a same-region latest-committed **item** read. It is not Cassandra `QUORUM`. It is not Raft. Do not use the three names interchangeably.

</NonNegotiable>

<Options title="Three write-coordination classes">

1. **Linearizable consensus log (Raft/Paxos-class).** Single ordered command stream: TokenX ledger mutations, lock/lease metadata, a chat sequencer if you put seq on a Raft group. Three replicas: commit is 2-of-3 (leader plus either follower). One group is one hot leader. Keep the dataset small or shard by key into many groups. Cross-region majority adds WAN RTT to every commit.
2. **Dynamo-style quorum store.** Partition-key items, tunable W/R, high write availability. Session blobs, shopping carts, Ad Click campaign metadata. `W=ONE` stays available and can lose a minority. `W=ALL` does the opposite. `QUORUM` is still not linearizable. Conditional writes occupy a key; they do not make default quorum a single-copy ledger.
3. **CRDT / last-write-wins.** Concurrent edits with a meaningful join: grow-only tags, presence last-seen, dashboard labels. Both sides of a partition write; merge or LWW runs after heal. Illegal for conservation, unique allocation, or security revocation that must monotonic-deny.

<InvalidatingFailure title="etcd as the TokenX ledger, or VIP as consensus">

The popular option is “etcd/Raft is linearizable, so put balances there” or “the load balancer only sends traffic to a healthy leader, so we have consensus.” Both fail.

etcd is a small linearizable KV for membership, elections, and fencing tokens. TokenX peak is on the order of thousands of durable transfers per second with multi-row conservation, history, and an outbox. That QPS and that schema overload a lease service: majority RTT on every debit, a log you cannot treat as a warehouse, and a failure domain shared with Kubernetes if you colocated them. etcd is the wrong store for TokenX balances at payment QPS.

A health-check VIP removes closed connections. It does not fence a stale primary, does not assign terms, and does not make a minority partition refuse writes. Two backends can pass HTTP health and both accept debits. That is split-brain with a green dashboard.

If you need one TokenX history, use a fenced ledger group (PostgreSQL-class with fencing, or a consensus/SQL ledger you will actually operate) and exclusive terminals. If you need available metadata, use quorum or CRDT **on objects that may converge**.

</InvalidatingFailure>

<OperationalBurden title="What the interview answer must staff">

- **Consensus:** quorum health, disk, snapshot, membership change as a protocol (joint consensus), epoch fencing. Three or five failure-independent nodes per group. Client timeout is UNKNOWN; retry the same idempotency key. Uncommitted suffix may be overwritten after election.
- **Dynamo-style quorum:** compaction, repair, tombstones, hinted handoff, capacity for celebrity keys. Managed offerings remove JVM toil, not the model. You must say W, R, N, and the repair policy in the interview.
- **CRDT/LWW:** permutation tests of merge, tombstone frontiers, replica/device identity growth, and a clock source you are willing to lose money on—which you should not be, for balances.

Leases expire. A lock service is not a mutex across partitions unless every writer checks the epoch.

</OperationalBurden>
